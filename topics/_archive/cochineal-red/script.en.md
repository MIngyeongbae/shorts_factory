# Cochineal — the red that cost almost as much as silver was an insect

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochineal
- 매체 적합성: Works — a hand rubbing white specks off a cactus until red bleeds across the fingers, heaps of dried insects, a cardinal's robe next to a British officer's coat, a map of cactus swallowing Australia, a lipstick ingredient list: the picture is the explanation
- 핵심 질문: What exactly was the red powder that all of Europe bought at prices second only to silver?
- 핵심 반전: That red was never a color meant for dyeing — it was a defensive chemical an insect carried inside its body so it wouldn't be eaten
- 보이는 것: white wax → a rub → a red stain, the color flipping on camera, and that same color moving from Aztec tribute sacks through European royal robes to today's ingredient labels
- 차별점: not a history of color, but the chain by which one insect's survival chemistry drags in imperial trade, espionage, and an Australian ecological disaster

## 대본

Would you believe that after silver, the most valuable export was a red powder?
Spain kept a monopoly on where it came from for nearly 300 years.
See that mold-like white patch on a cactus? Rub it and your hand turns bright red.
The white is a wax armor, and inside it is a deep purple insect.
It's called cochineal, a rice-grain-sized bug that lives on cactus sap.
But this red wasn't a color to begin with. It was a weapon.
A defense compound called carminic acid makes predators recoil.
The bug carried that color to say don't eat me — and people wore it.
Once an Aztec tax, this insect dyed cardinals' robes and British officers' coats.
But one pound of dye meant drying 70,000 insects.
So in 1777, a French scholar posed as a physician and stole the bugs.
Britain shipped whole cacti to Australia in 1788, but only the insects died.
The cacti that survived covered 260,000 square kilometers of eastern Australia.
What broke the price wasn't espionage — it was 19th-century synthetic dye.
That red is still there today, on lipstick and strawberry milk labels.
E120 on the ingredient list: the color of a bug once worth almost as much as silver.
