# The Money Nobody Printed — The Cigarette Currency of a POW Camp

- 시드: https://gwern.net/doc/economics/1945-radford.pdf
- 매체 적합성: Fits — the exchange boards hanging in every hut, prices quoted per single cigarette, the price line swinging each time parcels stop or flood in, thin hand-rolled cigarettes held next to machine-made ones: the screen shows all of it directly and the narration only has to ride on top
- 핵심 질문: With no state and no mint, why did cigarettes become money in the camp, and why did that money tear itself apart?
- 핵심 반전: Cigarettes broke *because* they became money — money you can smoke kept vanishing, and once hand-rolling pipe tobacco yielded more cigarettes than the official rate, the good ones disappeared from the market
- 보이는 것: barter → the notice board → per-cigarette price tags → a price graph swinging with the ration cycle → cigarettes getting thinner → an empty shop
- 차별점: first-hand observation by an economist who was inside as a prisoner. No labour, no production — and an entire market grew anyway
- 여운: Price never followed ethics

## 대본

With no state and no bank, how does money come into being at all?
A Second World War prison camp. Every Red Cross parcel held cigarettes too.
No fields, no factories, so production was 0. Only tastes differed.
A non-smoker swapped his cigarettes for chocolate, and trade began.
But whoever wants your jam won't happen to be holding butter.
They needed a yardstick for value, and the yardstick was the cigarette.
Uniform, slow to spoil, easy to split — a small kind of gold.
Every hut hangs a board: name, room number, wanted, offered.
Once prices were public, the whole camp settled on one rate.
But this money had a flaw. You could smoke it.
When parcels stopped, the money went up in smoke and prices crashed.
When hundreds of thousands arrived, prices shot up instead.
Boom and slump ran on the ration schedule.
Then came pipe tobacco. One ounce counted as 25 cigarettes,
but rolled again by hand, an ounce made thirty.
Everyone unrolled the machine-mades, and the good cigarettes vanished.
Thin ones got refused, and the yardstick itself had bent.
The day the gates opened, goods poured in and a cigarette was just a cigarette again.

- [줄 1] A rhetorical question — no new factual claim
- [줄 2] Cigarettes were part of the Red Cross parcel, and every man received the same parcel — **confirmed** (same sources as lines 3 and 4)
- [줄 6] **Confirmed** — cigarettes rose from an ordinary commodity to the status of currency. The German mark (RMk) existed but, since there was almost nothing to buy at the canteen, it circulated only for settling gambling debts — "Introduction", "The Development and Organisation of the Market". The hook's "no state" is rhetoric for 'there was no issuing authority'; state money itself did exist (twist note 1)
- [줄 2] **Confirmed** — the setting is German and Italian POW camps in the Second World War (the camps the author experienced were officers' camps, Oflags, which is why the detaining power made no payments for work), and Red Cross food parcels were issued equally and regularly per man. Private parcels were unequal, owing to the numbers despatched and the vagaries of the post — "Introduction"
- [줄 3] **Confirmed** — a market came into existence without labour or production. The motive for trade was differences in individual preference — "The Development and Organisation of the Market"
- [줄 4] **Confirmed** — the earliest form of trade was direct barter: a non-smoker exchanging his cigarette issue for a smoker's chocolate ration (about two weeks after capture, at a transit camp in Italy) — "The Development and Organisation of the Market"
- [줄 5] **Confirmed (interpretation)** — the limit of direct barter (what you want and what you offer must match on both sides) is not stated in those terms in the article. It is supporting narration borrowed from standard monetary economics; the article records only the outcome, that relative values came to be expressed in cigarettes rather than in each other. Kept, since the script does not attribute it to the author
- [줄 6] **Confirmed** — within a month the relative value of every commodity was expressed in cigarettes rather than in other goods, and the cigarette became the standard of value — "The Development and Organisation of the Market"
- [줄 7] **Confirmed** — cigarettes were homogeneous, reasonably durable, and of convenient size for the smallest transaction or, in packets, for the largest, and so performed all the functions of a metallic currency: unit of account, measure of value, and store of value — "The Cigarette Currency"
- [줄 8] **Corrected** — the 'Exchange and Mart' notice board was posted in **every bungalow (hut)**, not in every room, under the headings name, room number, wanted, and offered. When a deal went through it was crossed off. The presence of "room number" among the headings is itself evidence that one board covered several rooms — "The Development and Organisation of the Market" (script line 8: "every room" → "every hut")
- [줄 9] **Confirmed** — the public, semi-permanent record of transactions made cigarette prices well known and tending to equality throughout the camp. This holds for the author's Oflag; at Stalag VIIA (Moosburg), with up to 50,000 prisoners, movement between compounds was restricted, so price differences and arbitrage persisted — "The Development and Organisation of the Market"
- [줄 10] **Confirmed** — the decisive defect of the cigarette was its strong non-monetary demand: people actually wanted to smoke it — "The Cigarette Currency"
- [줄 11] **Confirmed** — when the weekly issue of 25 or 50 cigarettes per man was interrupted, stocks soon ran out, prices fell, trading declined in volume, and the camp drifted back toward barter (deflation) — "The Cigarette Currency"
- [줄 12] **Confirmed** — private cigarette parcels trickled in all year, but the big numbers came quarterly, when the Red Cross received its allocation of transport; several hundred thousand cigarettes might arrive within a fortnight, prices soared, and then began to fall — "The Cigarette Currency"
- [줄 13] **Corrected** — the price cycle turned on the issue schedule of cigarettes and, to a lesser extent, of food, and most of the camp's economic troubles came from that fundamental instability. The driver the original names is not the postal schedule but the weekly issue and the quarterly transport allocation — "The Cigarette Currency", "Price Movements" (script line 13: "postal schedule" → "ration schedule")
- [줄 14] **Confirmed** — when the Red Cross issued pipe tobacco in lieu of cigarettes, it did so at a rate of 25 cigarettes to the ounce, and that rate was standard in exchanges — "The Cigarette Currency"
- [줄 15] **Confirmed** — the same ounce would produce 30 home-made cigarettes — "The Cigarette Currency"
- [줄 16] **Confirmed** — so people broke down machine-made cigarettes and re-rolled the tobacco, and the real cigarette virtually disappeared from the market. The author describes this as Gresham's Law at work — "The Cigarette Currency"
- [줄 17] **Confirmed** — hand-rolled cigarettes were not homogeneous, so pricing became unstable: each cigarette was examined before it was accepted, and thin ones were rejected or extra was demanded as a make-weight ("all the inconveniences of a debased currency") — "The Cigarette Currency"
- [줄 18] **Confirmed** — on 12 April 1945, with the arrival of elements of the 30th U.S. Infantry Division, an age of plenty made economic organisation and activity themselves redundant — "Conclusion" (the seed PDF's `i2th April` is an OCR error; re-extraction of the text confirms `12th April`.
