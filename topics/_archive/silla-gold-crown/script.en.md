# Silla's gold crown — in the tomb, it wasn't on the head but over the face

- 시드: https://namu.wiki/w/신라%20금관
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — the 出-shaped uprights and the antlers, gold sheet as thin as foil buckling under its own weight, the excavation layout with the crown down over the face, even the gold-silver alloy composition table (80.3~89.2wt%): the visuals carry the whole argument
- 핵심 질문: Was the Silla gold crown a crown a king actually wore, or something laid over the face after death?
- 핵심 반전: The composition overturns the "too thin to wear" argument — deliberately alloying it with silver to stiffen it is itself a trace of "made to be worn," so the answer may not be either/or
- 보이는 것: the crown's structure (rising branches, antlers) → a cross-section of the thin gold sheet → the excavation layout with the crown over the face → pure gold vs. silver alloy compared for hardness → two scenes splitting into ritual in life and burial in death
- 차별점: 出-shaped uprights with antlers attached is Silla's alone, and the observation that it comes out of the ground covering the face collides head-on with the fact that it's a silver alloy — inside the same artifact

## 대본

They say a king wore this Silla gold crown — but in the tomb, it lay as if over his face.
So is this a crown, or a mask for the dead?
Branch-shaped uprights rise like the character 出, and antlers sit behind them.
But this gold sheet is as thin as foil.
Stand it up and it buckles on its own — it won't even hold its shape.
Someone walked around wearing that? Hence the death mask theory.
A funeral covering, laid over the dead person's face.
In 2020, in Hwangnam-dong, Gyeongju, a gilt-bronze crown was found down over the face too.
But then they analyzed what the crown was made of, and the story flips.
Not pure gold — an alloy, with silver mixed in on purpose.
Pure gold is soft enough to dent if you bite it.
Add silver and it gets much harder, and it keeps its shine.
Here the Gyeongju National Museum's special exhibition asks back.
Why alloy something you drape over a face once and bury?
Building it so it wouldn't bend means they meant to wear it.
So the answer may not be one or the other.
Worn in ritual while they lived, then laid over that same face in death.

## 주장

- [줄 1] At excavation the crown was found pressed flat, with the headband down around the jaw and shoulders rather than the forehead — the observational basis of the death mask theory (whether it actually covered the face is still disputed, hence "as if over") — view of Prof. Lee Han-sang, Daejeon University, https://v.daum.net/v/20260601004314360 / opposing: https://v.daum.net/v/20260526050651936
- [줄 2] Two readings of the crown's purpose compete: a state-ritual regalia theory and a funerary death mask theory. The death mask theory was the standard view for 70~80 years, going back to Hamada Kosaku in the colonial period, and entered reassessment with Sim Hyeon-cheol's (Keimyung University) direct rebuttal in 2025~2026 — https://v.daum.net/v/20260526050651936, https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003292082
- [줄 3] A Silla gold crown consists of three tree-branch uprights set on the headband in three tiers in the shape of 出, plus two antler-shaped uprights at the left and right rear — https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/신라_금관, https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0068699
- [줄 4] The crowns were cut from thin gold sheet (thickness figures dropped — no confirmed primary source) — https://www.museum.go.kr/MUSEUM/contents/M0501000000.do?schM=view&relicRecommendId=519954
- [줄 5] Structural fragility has been raised consistently as grounds that these were not worn in daily life — Sim Hyeon-cheol, "Reconsidering the Death Mask Theory of Silla Gold Crowns," Gomunhwa, 2025, https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003292082
- [줄 6] That they were too thin and weak for a person to wear and walk properly is a main plank of the death mask theory (counter: weights of 692g~1262.6g, comparable to a 1.1kg service combat helmet) — the paper above, https://www.hankyung.com/article/202511055796i, https://v.daum.net/v/388s6PaeTO
- [줄 7] The death mask theory treats the crown as a funerary object covering the dead person's face. The occupant was not necessarily an adult male king (Tomb 120-2 at Hwangnam-dong held a female aged 12~15) — the paper above, https://www.khs.go.kr/newsBbz/selectNewsBbzView.do?newsItemId=155705016&sectionId=b_sec_1&mn=NS_01_02
- [줄 8] In September 2020, at Tomb 120-2 in Hwangnam-dong, Gyeongju, a gilt-bronze crown, gilt-bronze shoes and gold earrings were confirmed as worn, and the occupant's lower teeth turned up at the center and lower part of the gilt-bronze crown's headband, showing the crown had come down over the face (gilt-bronze, not gold) — https://www.khs.go.kr/newsBbz/selectNewsBbzView.do?newsItemId=155705016&sectionId=b_sec_1&mn=NS_01_02
- [줄 9~10] Compositional analysis shows all six Silla gold crowns are gold-silver alloys; upright sheet purity runs Gyodong 89.2 / Hwangnamdaechong North Mound 86.2 / Geumgwanchong 85.4 / Cheonmachong 83.5 / Geumnyeongchong 82.8 / Seobongchong 80.3wt% (about 19.3~21.4K) — Shin Yong-bi et al., "Analysis of the Composition of Silla Gold Crowns," Conservation Science in Museum 16, 2015, https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO201514862252429.pub?lang=ko&orgId=museum
- [줄 11] Gold's Mohs hardness is 2.5~3; pure gold is soft enough that biting it leaves a mark — https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/금, https://www.hankyung.com/article/202511055796i
- [줄 12] Silver was added deliberately to offset pure gold's softness, raising strength while keeping the luster — https://www.hankyung.com/article/202511055796i
- [줄 13~15] The exhibition and catalogue argument of the 2025~2026 Gyeongju National Museum special exhibition "Silla Gold Crowns: Power and Prestige" (Nov 2025~Feb 22, 2026, six gold crowns): there would be no reason to add a strength-raising alloying step to something worn once and buried, and the continuous-dot (ring-dot) pattern also stiffens the sheet so the uprights don't fold — so they read it as made with actual wear in mind (leading theory) — https://www.hankyung.com/article/202511055796i, https://www.museum.go.kr/MUSEUM/contents/M0501000000.do?schM=view&relicRecommendId=519954, https://www.kbmaeil.com/article/20251126500497
- [줄 16~17] There is no need to force a life-use/funeral-use dichotomy; a crown used in ritual during life may have been reused in the funeral rites as a covering over the face (leading theory, not settled) — https://www.hankyung.com/article/202511055796i
