# The People in the Bamboo Control Tower — What They Actually Wanted

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_cult
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — the runway hacked out of the jungle, the straw planes and the bamboo control tower are themselves the evidence for the argument, so the footage does the explaining
- 핵심 질문: Why would people who had seen real planes with their own eyes build a control tower out of bamboo?
- 핵심 반전: They didn't misunderstand the technology — the leading explanation is that in a society where wealth only becomes power once it's given away, this was a demand to reopen trade with people who wouldn't share
- 보이는 것: a runway splitting the jungle → a full-size straw plane and a bamboo antenna → big-man exchange, gifts moving both ways → military planes pouring out supplies → the empty runway after the war
- 차별점: the detail that Japanese forces knew the belief in advance and posed as the ancestors while handing out goods
- 여운: what control tower are we sitting in right now

## 대본

Why did islanders keep waiting decades for planes that were never coming?
In the South Pacific after the war, people actually tried it.
Islanders in Melanesia cut a runway straight out of the jungle.
They wove full-size planes from straw and raised bamboo antennas.
We were taught this was primitive people misreading civilization.
But anthropologists say that reading is the wrong one.
On these islands, wealth wasn't something you piled up. It was something you gave away.
The man who gave the most stood the highest. That's a "big man."
And the white men never shared their mountains of cargo.
So a story spread. That cargo was sent by our ancestors, for us.
And the white men intercepted it somewhere along the way.
Japanese forces even knew the belief, and posed as the ancestors.
Handing out supplies to buy the islanders' labor.
So that control tower wasn't only calling in planes.
The leading explanation: it was an invitation to reopen a broken trade.
The man sitting in that bamboo control tower wasn't only waiting for planes.
He was calling back a trading partner who had disappeared.
