# There were never 38 witnesses — the number came from a single sheet of paper

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Kitty_Genovese
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — the floor plan of the Kew Gardens building and the two attack sites, the 1964 newspaper front page, and the police interview record listing 49 people show directly, in pictures, why nobody could see the whole thing and how 38 was manufactured
- 핵심 질문: Where did that number everyone knows, 38, actually come from?
- 핵심 반전: 38 appears to be not a headcount of witnesses but the number of entries in the police's first interview record (Kevin Cook's document check) — 49 people were listed, and two or three people living in the same apartment were folded into one entry
- 보이는 것: the building's bent layout and the two separate attack sites, sightlines cropped window by window, 49 people folded into 38 lines on a form
- 차별점: not the crime but the genealogy of a number — how the line count on one sheet of paper ruled psychology textbooks for 40 years
- 여운: the number was corrected; the story stayed

## 대본

The story goes that 38 neighbors watched a murder and did nothing.
New York, 1964. Kitty Genovese is murdered outside her own building.
Two weeks later, the New York Times front page settles it: nobody called.
As proof that cities are cold, it filled psychology textbooks for 40 years.
But that number, 38 — where did it actually come from?
Years later, the writer Kevin Cook dug up the police's first interview records.
The people listed there weren't 38. There were 49.
Two or three people living in one apartment were folded into a single entry.
So 38 wasn't a count of witnesses. It looks like it was a count of lines.
And there were two attacks — one on the street, one in a hallway behind the building.
Each window caught one different frame of the same film.
Only two people realized she had been stabbed.
One neighbor shouted and drove the attacker off; others did call the police.
Sophia Farrar held Kitty in her arms as she was dying.
When a TV reporter pressed him on it, Gansberg said, "It would have ruined the story."
In 2016, the Times itself admitted it had grossly exaggerated the story.
Those 38 witnesses only ever existed on paper.
