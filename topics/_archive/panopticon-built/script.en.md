# The Panopticon — how a prison that was never built became the word for surveillance

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panopticon
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — Reveley's circa-1791 plan and section, the radial floor plan of Pentonville, and interior photographs of Cuba's Presidio Modelo each stage the question of sightlines — who can see how far — in a single image.
- 핵심 질문: How did a building that was never built once become the word for surveillance?
- 핵심 반전: What Bentham designed wasn't surveillance — it was the state of not knowing whether you're being watched. That state copies itself without walls, so every building failed and only the idea survived.
- 보이는 것: Sightlines fanning out from a central tower, a tower whose blinds hide what's inside, the peephole at Pentonville you have to walk a corridor to reach, Cuba's circular block filling with trash
- 차별점: Instead of introducing the Panopticon as "the symbol of the surveillance society," it follows the lineage of failure — not one was built in Bentham's lifetime, and the buildings that imitated it couldn't even manage surveillance
- 여운: The tower works even when it's empty

## 대본

The round prison that became a byword for surveillance was never built.
Not one, not in the whole lifetime of its designer, Jeremy Bentham.
It began in Russia in 1786, in the workshop of his younger brother Samuel.
One man keeping an eye on the entire workforce, all day long.
The older brother moved it into a prison: a tower at the centre, cells all around.
The real device wasn't the tower. It was the blinds fitted to the tower.
The guard can see the prisoners. The prisoners can't see into the tower.
So they assume they're always visible, and they start policing themselves.
He didn't add more watchers. He planted a guard inside your head.
Bentham staked his own fortune, and the British government still refused.
The prisons built afterwards were shells that only looked the part.
At Pentonville, guards had to walk the corridor and squint through a peephole.
Cuba built it properly — and 6,000 men were packed in with the trash.
The philosopher Foucault called it a diagram, not a building.
Once you can make someone unsure, you don't need the walls.
In 2026 the UK Home Secretary said AI would finally achieve it.
That prison was never built. Now there's no need to build it.
