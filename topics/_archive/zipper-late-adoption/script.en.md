# 86 Years to Reach Your Pants — the Zipper Was Aiming at Shoelaces

## 대본

The zipper was never meant to go on clothes.
In 1851, sewing machine inventor Elias Howe patented the first one — more drawstring than fastener, never sold.
42 years later, Judson picked it up again — aiming at shoelaces.
He showed it at the Chicago World's Fair. Nobody bought it.
The hooks kept slipping out of line and jamming — sometimes breaking.
There were only four hooks per inch, far too sparse.
13 years later, Swedish-American electrical engineer Gideon Sundback joins Judson's company.
He swaps the hooks for teeth and packs eleven into one inch.
Then he gives each tooth a raised bump and a hollow.
Like stacked spoons, one tooth's bump nests into the next tooth's hollow.
And only the slider's Y-shaped channel can pry that ledge open.
So no tug while you're wearing it can pull it open.
1913: it finally sells — on boots and tobacco pouches.
10 years later it goes on Goodrich's rubber boots, closing in one long zip.
The company named it after that sound: zipper.
Its showdown with buttons over the trouser fly came in 1937.
Something never meant for clothes took 86 years to reach your pants.

- [줄 1] The zipper's ancestor started as a shoe fastener, not a garment fastener; Judson's device was not used in clothing — 출처: Wikipedia "Zipper" § History ("his device was not used in clothing")
- [줄 2] In 1851 Elias Howe received "Improvement in Fastenings for Garments" (US patent 8,540, November 25, 1851). But the device was "more like an elaborate drawstring than a true slide fastener," and he did not seriously pursue marketing it — 출처: § History. ※ Wikipedia only speculates that this was "perhaps because of the success of his sewing machine"; Howe won his sewing machine patent suit and collected royalties from 1854, after 1851 (Wikipedia "Elias Howe")
- [줄 2] Howe was the sewing machine inventor who patented the lockstitch machine in 1846 (patent 4,750) — 출처: Wikipedia "Elias Howe"
- [줄 3] 42 years after Howe, in 1893, Whitcomb Judson received "Shoe-Fastening" (patent 504,037) and "Clasp Locker or Unlocker for Shoes" (504,038) on August 29. The goal was replacing boot laces — 출처: § History
- [줄 4] Judson showed it at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair but had almost no commercial success — 출처: § History, Smithsonian Libraries blog. ※ Some research notes that no listing in the fair's official catalog has been confirmed, so on-screen labels stay at "shown at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair"
- [줄 5] Early prototypes worked but had mechanical problems that resulted in jamming — 출처: § Mechanism. ※ "sometimes breaking" simplifies the description that a zipper failure appears as jamming or partial breakage (§ Description: "it can either jam or partially break off") and applies it to the early device
- [줄 5·6] Judson's device used hook-and-eye elements, not teeth. The script calls them "hooks" — 출처: § History ("a (more complicated) hook-and-eye shoe fastener")
- [줄 6] Judson-type devices had 4 fastening elements per inch (about 6.4mm apart) — 출처: § History, National Inventors Hall of Fame. ※ The causal claim that raising the element count was the key to reliability is supported by the NIHF account
- [줄 7] 13 years after 1893, in 1906, Swedish-American electrical engineer Gideon Sundback was hired by the company founded by Judson and Walker (Universal Fastener → later Talon) — 출처: § History
- [줄 8] Sundback dropped the hooks (the 1913 product was named "Hookless Fastener") and raised the fastening elements from 4 per inch to 10 or 11 (about 2.5mm apart) — 출처: § History, National Inventors Hall of Fame
- [줄 9] [줄 10] The teeth in Sundback's patent (1,219,881) have an external projection and an internal recess, and one member nests within the recess of its neighbor into a snug, stable state — 출처: § Mechanism
- [줄 11] The slider meets the teeth one at a time through a Y-shaped channel, opening and closing an elastic "contractible jaw" to lock and unlock reversibly — 출처: § Mechanism
- [줄 12] The shape and size of the teeth are designed so that the forces acting on the zipper while the garment is worn cannot unlock them — 출처: § Mechanism ("the forces which act on the zipper when the garment it is sewn on is worn cannot unlock the teeth")
- [줄 13] The zipper first sold in 1913, and its two main early uses were closing boots and tobacco pouches — 출처: § History
- [줄 14] 10 years after 1913, in 1923, the B. F. Goodrich Company used Sundback's fastener on its new rubber boots, which closed with a single "zip." Zipper is onomatopoetic — 출처: § History, § Description
- [줄 15] The North American name "zipper" came from the B. F. Goodrich Company in 1923 and stuck — named by a company, not by the inventor — 출처: § History
- [줄 16] In the 1937 "Battle of the Fly" the zipper beat the button, and French fashion designers praised zippers on men's trousers — 출처: § History. ※ Talon's "Newest Tailoring Idea for Men" ad ran in the first issue of Esquire, published October 1933, and must be kept separate from the 1937 events
- [줄 17] 86 years from Howe's first patent in 1851 to adoption on the trouser fly in 1937 — 출처: § History, counting the years 1851 and 1937
