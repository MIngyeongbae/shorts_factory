# Why a Japanese city decides when it's noon in Korea

- 시드: https://namu.wiki/w/한국%20표준시
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — three meridians drawn on a map (120, 127.5 and 135 degrees east) with the peninsula sitting between them, a timeline of the line jumping in 1908, 1912, 1954 and 1961, and a Seoul-versus-Tokyo sunrise comparison all carry the explanation directly
- 핵심 질문: Why does the meridian that sets Korea's clocks run through Akashi in Japan's Hyogo Prefecture instead of through Korea itself?
- 핵심 반전: Running on someone else's meridian looks like a loss, but it hands Korea 30 minutes of extra evening light all year, with no daylight saving time
- 보이는 것: the reference line sliding off the peninsula, clock hands sitting 30 minutes out of step with the sun, the gap in brightness outside a Seoul window and a Tokyo window at the same hour
- 차별점: the habit of subtracting 30 minutes for a fortune reading, and the 1961 switch pushed through for the convenience of US Forces Korea — geography, history and daily rhythm stacked inside a single clock

## 대본

The time on Korea's clocks right now is set from somewhere that isn't Korea.
Korea's reference meridian runs through Akashi, in Japan's Hyogo Prefecture.
In world time, Seoul's real hour is 8 hours and 28 minutes ahead.
So Korea's clocks run about 30 minutes ahead of the sun.
That's why Korean fortune tellers often shave 30 minutes off a birth time.
It wasn't always this way. In 1908 a Korean Empire decree set the line.
127.5 degrees east — through Cheongju and Suncheon, straight down the peninsula.
Then in 1912 the Government-General of Korea moved it to Japan's 135.
In 1954 the Rhee government moved it back. 7 years later, back again.
This time, they say, it was US Forces Korea wanting easier operations.
So is Korea just losing out, wearing somebody else's clock?
A clock 30 minutes fast means the sun rises and sets 30 minutes later.
Like someone who sets their watch ahead because they hate being late.
So summer evenings stay bright that much longer, with no daylight saving.
Winter mornings are the price. Tokyo is bright while Seoul is still dark.
So today, again, Korea's noon is decided in Akashi.
30 minutes taken away — and a summer evening thrown in for free.

## 주장

- [줄 1~2] The 135th meridian east, the reference meridian for Korea Standard Time (UTC+9), passes through Japan rather than the Korean peninsula, and among the cities on that line Akashi in Hyogo Prefecture stands as the representative "city of time" — 확인 — https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本標準時子午線 · https://www.am12.jp/135-jstm/
- [줄 3] Seoul (127 degrees east) sits at UTC+8:28 by mean solar time — 확인 — https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/한국_표준시
- [줄 4] Korea's clocks run about 32 minutes ahead of Seoul's mean solar time (exactly 30 minutes in terms of meridian spacing), giving a year-round effect equivalent to 30 minutes of daylight saving. The script says "about 30 minutes" — 정정 반영 — https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/한국_표준시
- [줄 5] Subtracting 30 minutes from a birth time is a widespread practice in saju readings but not a rule shared by every school, and people born between 1954 and 1961 are not affected because the standard time then was already UTC+8:30. The script softens this to "often" — 정정 반영 — https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002985374
- [줄 6] On 1 April 1908, Korean Empire Decree No. 5 (Official Gazette No. 3994) first adopted 127.5 degrees east (GMT+08:30). But the decision was made under the Residency-General after the 1905 treaty, and the Residency-General had been directing its offices to use Japanese standard time since 1906, so any wording implying Korea "picked it freely" is removed — 정정 반영 — https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/한국_표준시 · https://www.newstopkorea.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=708
- [줄 7] The 127.5-degree line crosses the central spine of the peninsula, through Gapyeong, Yangpyeong, Icheon, Cheongju, Geumsan, Jinan, Namwon, Gurye and Suncheon — 확인 — https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/동경_127.5도
- [줄 8] On 1 January 1912, Government-General of Korea Gazette No. 367 (Notice No. 338) changed it to 135 degrees east (GMT+09:00), the same as Japan — 확인 — https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/한국_표준시
- [줄 9] On 21 March 1954, Presidential Decree No. 876 restored 127.5 degrees (GMT+08:30); at 00:00 on 10 August 1961 (Act No. 676, promulgated 7 August by the Supreme Council for National Reconstruction) it went back to 135 degrees east — 확인 — https://www.khan.co.kr/article/201008092147425 · https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/한국_표준시
- [줄 10] The official reason given for the 1961 change was "irrationality in aviation, navigation, astronomical and meteorological observation," but the factor reported as decisive was a request from US Forces Korea, citing operational disruption caused by the time gap with US forces stationed in Japan — 정정 반영 — https://www.khan.co.kr/article/201008092147425
- [줄 12] Switching to UTC+08:30 would move both sunrise and sunset 30 minutes earlier on the clock; under the current UTC+9 the sun therefore rises and sets that much later (Seoul at the summer solstice: 05:11/19:57 → 04:41/19:27) — 확인 — https://api.sunrise-sunset.org/json?lat=37.5665&lng=126.9780&date=2026-06-21&tzid=Asia/Seoul&formatted=0 · https://astro.kasi.re.kr/life/pageView/6
- [줄 14] Korea already runs on a time zone ahead of its mean solar time, so it gets a de facto daylight-saving effect without any daylight saving time — 확인 — https://www.newstopkorea.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=708
- [줄 15] At the same hour on a winter morning Tokyo is already light while Seoul is still dark (winter solstice sunrise: Tokyo 06:45, Seoul 07:41 — a 56-minute gap). The two cities differ by about 12.7 degrees of longitude, putting solar noon roughly 51 minutes apart — 확인 — https://api.sunrise-sunset.org/json?lat=35.6895&lng=139.6917&date=2026-12-21&tzid=Asia/Tokyo&formatted=0 · https://api.sunrise-sunset.org/json?lat=37.5665&lng=126.9780&date=2026-12-21&tzid=Asia/Seoul&formatted=0
- [줄 16~17] Korea Standard Time today is UTC+9, based on 135 degrees east, a reference introduced under Japanese colonial rule. But the reason it has held for some 60 years is judged to be pragmatic rather than a failure to clear colonial legacies: fewer than 5% of countries worldwide use half-hour offsets, and the current setting works as de facto daylight saving — 확인 — https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/한국_표준시 · https://www.newstopkorea.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=708
