## 대본

Why did America try to cover the Statue of Liberty in paint?
On dedication day in 1886, the statue wasn't green at all.
She gleamed a reddish brown, like a freshly minted coin.
Her whole body was wrapped in a thin sheet of copper.
That copper is 2.4 millimeters thick — about as thick as a nickel.
But just after 1900, the red started slowly slipping away.
Green stains spread, and by 1906 they covered her completely.
Officials read this as corrosion. The copper was rotting.
Congress even approved money to paint her inside and out.
Then the public rose up. Leave that color exactly as it is.
So the Army Corps of Engineers studied the green itself.
The finding was the opposite: the green layer was protecting the copper.
Air, water, and acid in the smoke had reacted with copper to form a film.
Like a scab over a scratch, it kept deeper corrosion out.
In the end, the paint went only on the inside, where nobody sees it.
The color they nearly painted over is the face we know today.
