## 대본

Leave one broken window, the saying goes, and the whole city comes down.
The evidence for that was a single experiment, half a century ago.
In 1969, psychologist Zimbardo abandoned two cars.
One in the Bronx, one in wealthy Palo Alto.
The Bronx car was stripped within minutes — the first takers, a family.
Within 24 hours everything of value was gone. Just a shell.
And the Palo Alto car? Untouched for over a week.
So Zimbardo picked up a sledgehammer and smashed it himself.
Then people joined in — the first ones, reportedly, his own students.
Disorder didn't spread on its own. The scene was staged.
13 years later, Wilson and Kelling publish "Broken Windows."
One pillar held up their claim that disorder breeds crime.
What stuck was a line: one window invites a thousand.
On those words New York cracked down on fare-beating and graffiti,
but crime fell too in cities that ran different policies.
In 2005, in Lowell, researchers split the worst blocks in two.
The cleaned-up half saw calls drop 20% — better than arrests.
A researcher swung the first hammer; a broom fixed the window.
