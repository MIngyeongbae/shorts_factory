# Jongmyo Jeongjeon — nobody designed it to be this long

- 시드: https://namu.wiki/w/종묘
- 매체 적합성: Fits — the growth from five chambers to nineteen, columns older on the west and newer on the east, a roofline that only ever grew sideways — the picture is the argument
- 핵심 질문: Why did Korea's longest wooden building stop at exactly this length?
- 핵심 반전: 101 meters isn't a design, it's an accumulation. The "it predicted the number of kings" story has the order backwards — it's simply where the dynasty ended and the additions stopped
- 보이는 것: five spots → 11 chambers → 15 → 19, a building that grows only sideways, with different bracket sets and different grain in each stretch
- 차별점: a rule bred an exception, the exception hardened into habit, and the building got physically pushed outward — a shape made by institutions

## 대본

Nobody ever set out to build this building this long.
Jongmyo Jeongjeon, 101 meters. The longest wooden building in Korea.
At the start, all that fit inside was five spirit tablets.
The rule was simple. After five generations, a king vacates his room.
Joseon moved him next door, into Yeongnyeongjeon.
Then came an exception: a king of great merit can't be moved.
Under Yeonsangun, Taejong was next out, and nobody dared touch him.
So Munjong got shoved into a side chamber, and the first permanent seat appeared.
The exception soon became the habit. Direct heirs mostly stayed.
Under Myeongjong they ran out of chambers, so they finally stretched the building.
It burned away in the Imjin War, and went back up at the same size.
Yeongjo added four chambers, Heonjong four more. That made 19.
Every chamber added is time added — less a building than a timeline.
Look at the columns and the ages differ. The west end is the oldest.
People call it a prophecy, filled perfectly to the very last chamber.
But that's only where the dynasty ended and the additions stopped.
101 meters nobody designed. A length Joseon pushed out.
