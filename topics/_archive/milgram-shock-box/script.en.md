# The Electric Chair With Its Wires Cut — What Milgram's Experiment Actually Measured

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milgram_experiment
- 매체 적합성: A good fit — the panel of 30 switches in 15-volt steps, the wall dividing two rooms, wiring that never reaches the chair, and the four-step ladder of prods are all structural pictures that carry the explanation
- 핵심 질문: Why did ordinary people reach all the way to the 450-volt switch?
- 핵심 반전: The machine and the sounds through the wall were both fake, and what pushed people to the end was not an order — at the fourth prod, the one that sounded most like a command, they pulled back instead; what held them was the justification, "the experiment requires it"
- 보이는 것: the row of switches climbing from 15 to 450 one step at a time, the turn where the sound through the wall becomes an actor in the next room, the gap between a prediction of one person and an actual 65 percent, four lines stacking up in order and collapsing at the last one
- 차별점: it shows that the name "the obedience experiment" is itself wrong, using only four lines of that experiment's own script
- 여운: The order that works best is the one that doesn't sound like an order.

## 대본

Of a hundred ordinary people, how many would push a lethal 450 volts into a stranger?
Yale, 1961. In front of the volunteer, thirty switches.
They climb one step at a time, from 15 volts to 450.
Every time the learner next door gets it wrong, the volunteer moves up a step.
There were no screams — at 300 volts, just pounding on the wall.
Then silence. But all of that sound was an act.
The learner was really an actor and the voltage was zero — the volunteer didn't know that.
Before it began, he had felt one real shock himself.
One in a hundred would go all the way — that was Yale students' prediction.
Instead, all 40 participants pressed 300 volts.
65 percent went to 450, the last switch on the panel.
Say you want to stop, and the experimenter has four set lines, in order.
Only the last is a real order: "You have no other choice."
But when it sounded like an order, people pulled their hands back.
What pushed them to the end was "The experiment requires you to continue."
The likeliest read: it wasn't obedience that moved them, it was a cause.
The wires were cut. The circuit that moved people was working fine.

## 주장

- [줄 1] A rhetorical question. That 450 volts is the top step rests on the same source as line 3, and "enough to kill" describes the voltage itself — **note**: the generator's plate ran from "Danger: Severe Shock" to "XXX", not "lethal" (seed, Mixon variation: obedience collapsed once the danger was spelled out). **It does not mean participants were told they might kill someone**
- [줄 7] The shock generator never delivered real electricity — Confirmed. "in reality there were no shocks" (도입부 / Procedure)
- [줄 2] The experiments began in 1961 at Yale University — Confirmed. "The experiments began in August 1961 at Yale University" (도입부). Some sources give July as the start month, but the year is the same
- [줄 2] There were 30 switches — Confirmed. "The instrument panel consists of 30 lever switches" (Milgram 1963, psy.miami.edu PDF)
- [줄 3] Voltage rose in 15-volt increments from 15 to 450 — Confirmed (Procedure)
- [줄 4] The learner was strapped into an electric chair in the adjacent room, and the teacher raised the shock one level for each wrong answer — Confirmed (Procedure)
- [줄 5] There were no screams — Correction applied. In the first (remote) condition that produced the 65% figure, no vocal protest was heard until 300 volts (Milgram 1963). Screams and pleas belong to the later voice-feedback variation (62.5%)
- [줄 5] The learner pounded on the wall at 300 and 315 volts — Confirmed (도입부)
- [줄 6] At the highest voltages the learner fell silent — Confirmed (Procedure)
- [줄 6] The sound through the wall was staged — Correction applied. The seed says it came from a tape recorder integrated with the generator, but the original paper describes the wall-pounding in the first experiment as made by the actor in the next room — so not asserted as "all recorded," changed to "an act"
- [줄 7] The learner was an actor and a confederate of the experimenter — Confirmed (Procedure)
- [줄 7] The voltage applied to the learner was zero — Correction applied. Current is measured in amperes, not volts; rewritten as "the voltage he got"
- [줄 8] Before the main run, the teacher himself was given a real sample shock — Confirmed. "the teacher was given a sample electric shock" (Procedure, 원논문 45볼트)
- [줄 9] Fourteen Yale senior psychology majors predicted that on average only 1.2 in 100 would go to the maximum voltage — Confirmed (Predictions). The script rounds this to "one"
- [줄 10] All 40 participants continued to 300 volts — Confirmed (도입부 / Results)
- [줄 11] 65% (26/40) went to 450 volts — Confirmed (도입부). Perry's reassessment ("only half believed it was real") is a contested minority view and is not used
- [줄 12] When a subject said he wanted to stop, the experimenter used four set verbal prods in order — Confirmed (Procedure, 프로토콜 기준)
- [줄 12] The one delivering the prods was the "experimenter" — Correction applied. The grey-coated experimenter was played by John Williams, a high school biology teacher, so "researcher" was replaced with "experimenter"
- [줄 13] The fourth prod is "You have no other choice; you must go on" — Confirmed (Procedure)
- [줄 13] Only the last of the four is phrased as an order — Confirmed. "only the fourth one resembles a clear order" (Haslam & Reicher 2011)
- [줄 14] The closer a prod came to an order, the less likely it was obeyed — Confirmed. "on nearly every occasion that the fourth prod was used, participants responded by refusing to continue" (Haslam et al. 2015)
- [줄 15] Obedience was higher when the prod stressed the scientific importance, as in "The experiment requires you to continue" — Confirmed (Alternative interpretations / Haslam·Reicher·Birney 2014)
- [줄 16] The "engaged followership" reading: participants were not obeying an order but trying to support the experimenter's scientific goals — Leading hypothesis (확정설 아님, rendered in the script as "the likeliest read")
