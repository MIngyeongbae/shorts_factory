# What Happened When Sushi Was Driven Off the Street

- 시드: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/寿司
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — The shift from disappearing Edo food stalls to indoor sushi counters can be clearly visualized.
- 핵심 질문: How did a street food become an expensive cuisine?
- 핵심 반전: Before its ingredients changed the price of sushi, the disappearance of its stalls did.
- 보이는 것: Edo street stalls, fish without refrigeration, hygiene regulations, dismantled stalls, and the transition to upscale restaurant counters
- 차별점: It explains sushi’s rise in status through a change in where it was sold, rather than through taste.

## 대본

When did sushi become an expensive course meal?
It’s easy to assume it was always an upscale craft.
But in the Edo period, sushi was sold on the street.
Fish was placed on hand-pressed rice and served right away.
Like fast food with no wait, it quickly spread across Edo.
The problem was handling fish on streets without refrigerators.
Sushi is made by touching both the fish and rice with bare hands.
Over time, even the warmth of the rice heated the fish.
Eventually, hygiene rules pushed sushi stalls off the streets.
By the early Showa period, most street sales involving fish were regulated.
Through the postwar high-growth era, the stalls disappeared.
Sushi moved off the street and into kitchens and behind counters.
A quick meal became a dish with seating and table service.
Then came market-priced fish and restaurants with no price tags.
It was like a street burger suddenly becoming a chef’s tasting course.
Sushi likely grew costly not from ingredients, but from losing the street.
