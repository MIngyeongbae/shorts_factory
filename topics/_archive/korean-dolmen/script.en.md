# Dolmens — the real reason half the world's are packed onto one peninsula

- 시드: https://namu.wiki/w/고인돌
- 매체 적합성: Good fit — the weight of the capstone, the crowd hauling it on logs and ropes, the cross-section of the burial chamber, the distribution map going solid black over South Jeolla and Gwangju: every causal step in the narration has a picture that carries it
- 핵심 질문: Why are half the world's dolmens crowded onto this one narrow peninsula?
- 핵심 반전: Dolmens aren't rocks piled up by cavemen — they're the product of a settled, rice-farming Bronze Age society, and one stone needs hundreds of people. So the count of dolmens is really a map of where people once lived packed together
- 차별점: Unlike other megalithic sites, the sheer number is overwhelming — and that number is itself the clue to how the society was organized

## 대본

Say "dolmen" and you picture a caveman — that image is wrong from the start.
The people who raised these stones were already planting rice in flooded paddies.
About half the world's dolmens are thought to sit on the Korean Peninsula.
Why crowd onto this narrow land? The answer is the weight of the stone.
At the Bugeun-ri dolmen in Ganghwa, one capstone is estimated at 80 tons.
Nobody had a crane back then. So how did they move it?
The leading explanation is logs laid underneath and ropes to haul it.
One ton takes ten adults. So 80 tons takes 800.
That's two entire middle schools today, emptied out for one stone.
But a band roaming after game can never gather that many people.
Numbers only pile up once you settle in one place and farm.
That's exactly why dolmens are Bronze Age relics.
So a place thick with dolmens is a place people were once packed into.
South Jeolla and Gwangju alone still hold some 20,000 of them.
Instead of a caveman, picture 800 people working a rice paddy.
Once that picture holds, the peninsula's dolmen count finally makes sense.
