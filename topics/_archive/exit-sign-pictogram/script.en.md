# The green man on the exit sign has a nationality

- 시드: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/非常口
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — put a lettered sign and a pictogram side by side, then overlay a cross-section of smoke banking down from the ceiling with the height the sign was mounted at, and "why couldn't anyone see it" lands before a word is spoken
- 핵심 질문: That green running man the whole world uses — who actually made him, and why?
- 핵심 반전: The picture didn't beat the word. Lettered signs failed twice, in smoke and in blackouts, and only then did Japan throw the letters out — and that replacement became the international standard
- 보이는 것: A ceiling sign that's lit and still invisible → a cross-section of a room filling with smoke → a panel where lettered signs swap to pictograms → countries with the green pictogram against America's red EXIT
- 차별점: This drawing isn't anonymous international design — it's the refined winner of a 1979 Japanese design competition

## 대본

Would you believe that little green man has a nationality?
In 1972, the Sennichi Department Store in Osaka caught fire.
The exit signs were lit. Every one of them. Nobody saw them.
Smoke had banked down from the ceiling, like oil spreading on water.
And the signs hung high up, right near the ceiling beams.
They had no battery either, so the blackout simply switched them off.
The next year in Kumamoto, the Taiyo store's signs were too small to spot.
Japan's conclusion was an odd one: get rid of the words entirely.
So in 1979, they ran a contest for a picture anyone could read.
Around 3,300 people entered, and the reworked winner is that running man.
Green was locked in — it was thought to stand out best against flames.
Two months before the rules took effect, the Hotel New Japan burned.
That shock helped too: 70% swapped over within 18 months of the notice.
In 1987, one added underline turned it into an ISO international standard.
No English needed, so Britain, Australia and Korea all use it.
In the US, plenty of buildings still run red EXIT lettering.
That green man's nationality is Japan — a face two fires made.

## 주장

- [줄 1] **확인** — The current exit pictogram (a running figure on green) was established in Japan and became an international standard — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」
- [줄 2] **확인** — The Sennichi Department Store fire broke out in Osaka in 1972 (13 May 1972, around 22:27; 118 dead) — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」/ ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/千日デパート火災
- [줄 3] **확인** — During the Sennichi fire, dense smoke and blackout darkness meant evacuees could not see the exit lights (ignition 22:27, all store lighting out at 22:49) — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」/ ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/千日デパート火災
- [줄 4] **확인** — "Smoke banks down from the ceiling" is smoke stratification. The seed article doesn't describe it, but hot smoke collecting most densely near the ceiling — degrading high-mounted signage first — is established fire engineering — Fire Technology (Springer), low-location egress marking standards material
- [줄 5] **근거 없음 → 정정** — The seed says only 「梁付近の高い位置」 (a high position near the beams); there is no basis for a superlative "highest." The superlative was removed from that line — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」
- [줄 6] **확인** — The exit lights of the time had no emergency power supply (battery) and went dark with the blackout — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」 (footnote: 「誘導灯の歴史 黎明期から現在まで」)
- [줄 7] **확인** — In the 1973 Taiyo Department Store fire in Kumamoto, the exit lights were too small for people to locate the exits, one factor in the death toll (29 Nov 1973; 104 dead) — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」 (footnote: 北後明彦「火災調査の歴史」神戸大学都市安全研究センター). Note the 大洋デパート火災 article also states the exit lights and other systems were mid-installation and not functional — size alone was not the cause
- [줄 8] **확인** — Buildings completed before 31 March 1982 used text-only markings such as 「非常口」「非常出口」「非常階段」, with no consistency between manufacturers in typeface or whether English was included — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」
- [줄 9] **확인** — A design competition was held in 1979 aiming at a sign anyone could recognize — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」
- [줄 10] **정정** — It is true that 小谷松敏文's entry was selected from roughly 3,300 submissions, but the selected entry is not the current design. It went through 太田幸夫's revision, was issued as a Fire Defence Agency notice on 20 Jan 1982 and took effect on 1 April. "the one picked" → "the reworked winner" — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」
- [줄 11] **유력설 → 정정** — The Fire Service Act's mandate of green for exit lights is confirmed, but the seed hedges the visibility claim as 「最も目立つ**とされ**」. The US NFPA 101 specifies no color, allowing red, orange or green. "because it's most visible" was softened to "it was thought to stand out best" (a judgment of its time) — 「概説」
- [줄 12] **확인** — The Hotel New Japan fire occurred on 8 Feb 1982, just before the rules took effect (33 dead). Measured to the 1 April start date it is precisely 52 days — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」/ nippon.com
- [줄 13] **정정** — The seed reads 「ホテルニュージャパン火災の影響**もあり**、**消防庁告示から**僅か1年半で、**70%以上**の誘導灯が新表示に取り換えられた」. (1) Causation compressed onto the fire alone was reopened as "that shock helped too"; (2) the 18-month clock starts from the 20 Jan 1982 notice, not the fire, now stated explicitly as "of the notice" — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」
- [줄 14] **확인** — In 1987 the design, with an underline added to the Fire Defence Agency notice version, was incorporated into ISO 6309:1987 as an international standard, and is also specified in ISO 7010:2003 (the sentence carries no seed footnote, but the standards themselves were verified in the official ISO catalogue) — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」/ iso.org/standard/12596.html
- [줄 15] **확인** — As of 2018, countries using a pictogram essentially equivalent to Japan's include the UK, Australia, Canada, Korea and New Zealand — 「各地域における誘導標識」
- [줄 16] **확인** — The US varies by state and municipality, with many using red or green EXIT lettering. New York City's 2014 building code specifies red EXIT signage only — 「各地域における誘導標識」
- [줄 17] **확인** — The fires that drove the pictogram's establishment are the two — Sennichi and Taiyo; the Hotel New Japan fire accelerated adoption rather than establishment. "A face two fires made" is accurate as to establishment — 「ピクトグラムの制定とISO標準化」
