# They blew up an island to build Yeouido, and it came back six times bigger

- 시드: https://namu.wiki/w/밤섬
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — 1966 aerial photos, a photo of the 1968 blast itself, the levee construction, and today's satellite imagery each prove "erased → came back" in a single frame
- 핵심 질문: How did an island dynamited off the map end up bigger than it was before?
- 핵심 반전: The blast didn't erase the island, it only stripped away the soil above the waterline — the bedrock left underwater became a snag that caught sand and grew the island back
- 보이는 것: before-and-after maps overlaid → Bamseom rock being stacked into the levee around Yeouido → cross-section of sand snagging on bedrock and piling up → area graph climbing from 45,000㎡ to 290,000㎡ → today's forest with migratory birds landing
- 차별점: land a city erased rebuilt itself in the middle of that same city, and became an internationally protected wetland that people are barred from entering

## 대본

They blew up an entire island, and it came back bigger.
This is Seoul in 1968, and Bamseom sat in the middle of the Han River.
Back then Yeouido was a sandbank that went under whenever it rained.
The city wanted to ring it with a levee and build a new town there.
But the Ministry of Construction blocked it. What about the floods?
The condition: keep the river 1,300 meters wide so floodwater could pass.
Sitting right in that channel was Bamseom, home to 443 people.
The residents moved across the river first, then the charges brought it down.
The broken rock was hauled off to build the levee around Yeouido.
But the explosives only took off the part of the island above water.
The hard bedrock on the riverbed stayed exactly where it was.
When it rains, the Han River swells and carries a huge load of sand.
That sand snagged on the leftover bedrock and piled up year after year.
It's the same way hair clumps together in a clogged drain.
In 1966 the island was 45,000 square meters. Today it's six times that.
Once people left, willows took over and migratory birds moved in.
In 2012 it joined the Ramsar list of protected wetlands worldwide.
The island they tried to erase grew, and now you need a permit to enter.

- [줄 4] **확인** — Seoul City (Mayor Kim Hyon-ok) planned to raise a levee called the Yunjungje and develop Yeouido into a modern city and secondary center — 서울&(한겨레)
- [줄 5] **확인** — The Ministry of Construction objected that "Bamseom seriously obstructs flood flow" and that the plan "could not withstand a 100-year flood" — 세종대왕신문 / 뉴스스페이스

- [줄 9] **확인** — Bamseom's rock was used as stone for the Yeouido levee, and its soil for landfill — 한국민족문화대백과사전 / 세종대왕신문
- [줄 10·11] **확인** — The blast removed only what was above the waterline; the bedrock and debris below it remained, and sediment began building on top — 한국민족문화대백과사전
