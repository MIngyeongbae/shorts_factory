## 대본

A question you would expect in a philosophy class got an answer from an international body.
Athens preserved the ship Theseus sailed home for centuries.
Pulling out the rotted planks, fitting fresh timber in their place, again and again.
Eventually the last plank goes. Not one piece of the original wood remains.
Then the philosopher Hobbes added a twist of his own.
He gathered the discarded old planks and built a second, identical ship.
Now there are two ships. Which one is the real ship of Theseus?
This is about you, too. In 80 days your body makes as many new cells as it has.
But there are people who have to rule on this for real.
The people who raise fallen monuments back up.
What UNESCO weighs isn't the number of original planks left.
It asks whether original material and solid documentation survived.
Hwaseong Fortress was badly destroyed, but its build record, the Hwaseong seongyeok uigwe, survived.
Brought back exactly as that record described, it became a World Heritage Site.
Bagrati Cathedral in Georgia was rebuilt on guesswork — and lost that status.
Swap every plank and the ship is still the ship. It disappears when the record does.
