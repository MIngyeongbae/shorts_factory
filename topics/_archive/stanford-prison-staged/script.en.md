# The Stanford Prison Experiment — those monsters were given acting notes

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_prison_experiment
- 매체 적합성: Fits — the original newspaper recruitment ad, the mirrored sunglasses and numbered smocks, the audio tapes, the moment prisoner 8612 breaks down. Every one of them goes on screen as evidence for the claim that this was staged
- 핵심 질문: That experiment where ordinary students turned cruel in 6 days — did the situation really change them?
- 핵심 반전: The cruelty didn't emerge on its own, the research team asked for it, and the most famous breakdown was later admitted to be acting by the man who performed it
- 보이는 것: The fake basement prison → mirrored sunglasses and numbered smocks → tapes playing back → a close-up of "prison life" in the newspaper ad. The same photographs get re-read, from lab record to film-set stills
- 차별점: This is an experiment where both things survive on the record: the researchers' own voices pushing a guard, and the participant saying "that was acting"
- 여운: That script is still being quoted.

## 대본

Ordinary college students put on uniforms and turned into monsters — that experiment.
In 1971, a fake prison goes up in a Stanford basement.
A coin toss split 24 students into guards and prisoners.
6 days in, they shut it down. The guards had gotten too cruel.
Not personality — situation. That was Professor Zimbardo's conclusion.
But the tapes recorded back then finally got a closer listen.
Playing warden was Jaffe, an undergrad assistant, and he pushed the guards.
Come down harder, for the good of the experiment. That's how he egged them on.
French researcher Thibault Le Texier dug up something worse.
The conclusions were largely written before the experiment began.
The most famous scene: prisoner 8612, broken after 36 hours.
In 2017, Douglas Korpi said it out loud. He was acting.
He faked the breakdown to get out and study for an exam.
Zimbardo pushed back, but most guards said they were acting too.
The recruitment ad called it a "study of prison life."
The people that phrase attracted scored unusually high on aggression.
Not observation — a stage, with a script and acting notes.
The uniform didn't make the monster. Someone directed one.

## 주장

- [줄 1] **확인** — Guards were issued deindividuating uniforms (khaki shirts and trousers from a military surplus store), mirrored sunglasses, and wooden batons, and the popular conclusion of the experiment was that roles and situations make ordinary people cruel — 시드: Preparation > Orientation, Interpretation and reproducibility of results
- [줄 2] **확인** — In August 1971 (8/14–8/21), a mock prison was built in a roughly 11m (35ft) stretch of basement corridor in Stanford's psychology building (Jordan Hall) — 시드: Preparation > Prison environment
- [줄 3] **확인** — 24 of 75 applicants were selected and assigned on a random basis to 9 guards plus 3 alternates and 9 prisoners plus 3 alternates — 시드: Preparation > Recruitment and selection
- [줄 4] **확인(부분)** — Planned for two weeks, it was halted on day 6, and escalating guard brutality was one of the reasons. The seed lists Maslach's objection and parents' concerns as parallel reasons (the script mentions only the brutality — see 반전 노트 3) — 시드: Events > Friday, August 20: Day 6
- [줄 5] **확인** — Zimbardo's interpretation was that the mock-prison situation, not individual personality, produced the participants' behavior — 시드: Interpretation and reproducibility of results
- [줄 6] **정정 반영** — The recordings were not newly released; they were already digitized and posted on the official SPE site, and were widely discussed in 2017 ("released" → "got attention") — 시드: Preparation > Roles
- [줄 7] **정정 반영** — David Jaffe, who played the warden, was not a researcher but an undergraduate research assistant ("researcher Jaffe" → "undergrad assistant Jaffe") — 시드: Preparation > Roles
- [줄 7~8] **확인** — Jaffe urged one guard to get more involved and act more "tough," justifying it as "for the benefit of the experiment" — 시드: Preparation > Roles
- [줄 9] **확인** — Thibault Le Texier is a French researcher who challenged the study's scientific validity in his 2018 book *Histoire d'un mensonge* and his 2019 American Psychologist paper "Debunking the Stanford Prison Experiment" — 시드: Critiques of scientific validity
- [줄 10] **정정 반영** — Le Texier argued that guards were directly asked to behave in specific ways and that the conclusions were "largely" written in advance. Not written entirely in advance, so "already written" → "largely written" (a point Zimbardo disputes) — 시드: Critiques of scientific validity
- [줄 11] **확인** — Prisoner 8612 showed signs of breakdown after 36 hours and was the first to leave, becoming the experiment's signature scene — 시드: Events > Prisoner 8612
- [줄 11~12] **확인** — Prisoner 8612 was Douglas Korpi, who said in a 2017 interview that his breakdown was faked — 시드: Events > Prisoner 8612
- [줄 13] **확인** — Korpi said he faked the breakdown in order to leave and study for the graduate school entrance exam (GRE) — 시드: Events > Prisoner 8612
- [줄 14] **확인** — Zimbardo responded that Le Texier's critique was largely ad hominem and ignored contrary data, and claimed Korpi's 2017 interview was a lie — 시드: Critiques of scientific validity, Events > Prisoner 8612
- [줄 14] **확인** — Most of the guards said afterward that they had been deliberately acting — 시드: Critiques of scientific validity
- [줄 15] **확인** — The recruitment ad's wording was "psychological study of prison life" ($15 a day, 1–2 weeks / Palo Alto Times, The Stanford Daily, August 1971) — 시드: Preparation > Recruitment and selection
- [줄 16] **확인** — Carnahan and McFarland (2007): people who answered an ad containing "of prison life" scored significantly higher on aggressiveness, authoritarianism, Machiavellianism, narcissism, and social dominance, and lower on empathy and altruism (a comparison study that re-recruited with the same wording, not a measurement of the 1971 participants) — 시드: Critiques of scientific validity, Similar studies
- [줄 18] **확인** — That the guards' brutality came from the research team's instructions and prompting rather than arising naturally from the situation is a point made by both Le Texier and the BBC prison study (Haslam and Reicher, run in 2002, published in 2006, with no behavioral guidance given to guards) — 시드: Critiques of scientific validity, BBC prison study
- [여운] **확인** — The experiment is still cited in films, documentaries, and course curricula (the 2015 film, the 2024 National Geographic three-part documentary *Unlocking the Truth*, and others) — 시드: Legacy > In popular culture
