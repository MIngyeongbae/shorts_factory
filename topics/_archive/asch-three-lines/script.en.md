# When everyone else insists on the wrong answer, the one person who keeps you standing

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asch_conformity_experiments
- 매체 적합성: Fits — the two line lengths on the cards, the participant seated in the last chair, and the moment unanimity breaks because of one person are all directly visible on screen, and the shifting numbers attach to the picture
- 핵심 질문: Why do people follow the majority even when the answer is obvious, and what makes them stop?
- 핵심 반전: What makes people fold isn't the *size* of the majority but the state of being unanimous — one dissenter breaks conformity, even a dissenter who gives an answer that's more wrong
- 보이는 것: The reference-line card and the A/B/C comparison card, seven people pointing to the same wrong line in turn while the real participant answers last, a conformity bar that stops rising as the opposing group grows to four and five, and the number bending from 35.7% to 5% the moment one ally walks in
- 차별점: The hidden half of an experiment remembered only for "the power of the majority" — the force of a single dissenter, and the 26% who never bent

## 대본

The answer is obvious, and the seven people ahead of you all pick the wrong one.
In 1951, psychologist Solomon Asch built exactly that room.
Two cards, and you name the line that matches the other in length.
Alone, people got it wrong less than 1% of the time.
The other seven in the room were actors, and the real subject answered last.
From the third round on, all seven point to the same wrong line.
The result? One in three answers went along with the majority.
So the more people, the scarier it gets? That's not what happened.
Three on the other side maxed out the pressure — more didn't add anything.
What broke people wasn't the number. It was the unanimity.
So Asch told one actor to give the correct answer every time.
With just one ally, the share still following the majority fell to 5%.
Here's the strange part: that one person didn't even have to be right.
Add someone picking a wilder wrong line, and it still dropped to 9%.
Unanimity is a pane of glass. Not thickness — one crack, and it's gone.
One in four participants never bent, not once across 12 rounds.
When seven ahead of you are wrong, one ally turns it around. It could be you.

## 주장

- [줄 1] In a group of eight, seven actors answered before the real participant, and alone people got the task wrong less than 1% of the time — **confirmed** · article "Method" / "Results"
- [줄 2] Solomon Asch ran the first conformity experiment in 1951 at Swarthmore College — **Confirmed** · article "Initial conformity experiment"
- [줄 3] The task was to look at a card with one reference line and a card with comparison lines A, B and C, and name the line of matching length — **Confirmed** · article "Method"
- [줄 4] In the control condition (37 people, alone with the experimenter, no actors), the error rate was under 0.7% — **Confirmed** · article "Results", Asch (1955) "less than 1 per cent"
- [줄 5] Of the 8-person group, 7 were actors, and seating was arranged so the real participant always answered last — **Confirmed** · article "Method"
- [줄 6] Of 18 total trials, the actors gave a unanimous wrong answer starting on the third, making 12 "critical trials" — **Confirmed** · article "Method"
- [줄 7] In the experimental condition, 35.7% of participant responses followed the actors' wrong answer (64.3% were correct) — **Confirmed** · article "Results". The often-quoted 74% uses a "conformed at least once" basis, so keep it off screen
- [줄 9] With a majority of 1 opposing, independence was high (3.6% errors); 2 gave 13.6%, 3 saturated at 31.8%, and going to 4 or more added nothing — **Corrected** · article "Majority size", Asch (1955) https://www.panarchy.org/asch/social.pressure.1955.html · the maximum is reached at 3, not 2, so "two or three on the other side" was changed to "three"
- [줄 10] "Unanimity, not size, is what matters" is an interpretation grounded in those two results (the saturation of the majority-size effect plus the sharp effect of one dissenter) — **Confirmed** · article "Majority size", "Presence of a true partner"
- [줄 11] Asch did in fact run a condition in which a single actor gave the correct answer every time — **Confirmed** · article "Method", "Presence of a true partner"
- [줄 12] In that condition only 5% of participants continued to answer with the majority — **Confirmed** · article "Presence of a true partner". Note that this figure traces to Morris & Miller (1975); Asch's own 1955 report describes errors "reduced to one-fourth" (≈9%)
- [줄 13–14] Even in the "extreme dissenter" condition, where the dissenter picks the most distant line rather than the correct one, the share answering with the majority fell to 9% — **Confirmed** · article "Partner dissent and accuracy"
- [줄 16] 26% of participants (≈one in four) never once conformed to the majority across all 12 critical trials — **Confirmed** · article "Results", Asch (1955) "about one quarter"
- [줄 15] The pane-of-glass image is an explanatory metaphor, not a factual claim — not subject to a verdict
