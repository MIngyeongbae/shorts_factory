# Who Added the Marathon’s Final 195 Meters?

## 대본

Why isn’t a marathon a neat 42 kilometers?
Those extra 195 meters hide a story more recent than antiquity.
It’s easy to assume the number was fixed from the start.
But early Olympic distances varied from one venue to another.
At the 1896 Athens Games, it was about 40 kilometers.
That distance followed a route from Marathon to Athens.
Then, in 1908 London, the course design got complicated.
It started at Windsor Castle and ended at White City Stadium.
Organizers first mapped out a road route of about 26 miles.
Then they placed the finish line in front of the royal box.
When the entrance changed, the track section shrank from one lap to 385 yards.
It was as if the royal seats became a ruler at the road’s end.
The official distance that day was therefore 42.195 kilometers.
But it didn’t become the worldwide standard right away.
Later Olympic marathons became shorter and longer again.
In 1921, the IAAF chose the London distance as the standard.
So the final 195 meters still carry the mark of the royal box.
