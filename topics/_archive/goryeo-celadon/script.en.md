# The color they called first under heaven, made without a drop of blue pigment

- 시드: https://namu.wiki/w/고려청자
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — a magnified glaze cross-section, the path of light bouncing off bubbles and scattering, the size gap between Gangjin's 10m kiln and China's 40m dragon kiln, and the crackle opening as clay and glaze shrink at different rates: every causal step can be shown on screen
- 핵심 질문: Not one grain of blue pigment went in, so why does celadon look blue?
- 핵심 반전: The blue came from iron in the clay and the glaze, but what made it deep and soft was bubbles trapped in the glaze — leftovers of a glaze that never fully melted, because small kilns fired fast and cooled fast. It started out as a defect.
- 보이는 것: the field of bubbles inside the glassy glaze → light scattering off them → Gangjin's 10m earthen kiln against China's 40m dragon kiln → crackle splitting open from the clay–glaze shrinkage gap
- 차별점: Iron made the color, but a defect made the masterpiece — a treasure built by accident, not by material

## 대본

There isn't one drop of blue pigment in the blue of Goryeo celadon.
No pigment at all — so where does this blue come from?
It's iron in the clay and glaze, giving up its oxygen.
But that deep, soft quality? That isn't the iron.
The glaze is a thin layer of glass, and it's packed with bubbles.
Light hits every one of those bubbles and scatters in all directions.
Even clear water turns cloudy the moment you foam it up.
That scattering wraps the iron's blue in something softer.
So did the potters put those bubbles in on purpose? No.
Gangjin's kilns ran about 10m; China's dragon kilns, 40m and up.
A small kiln heats up fast, and it cools down fast.
The glaze set before it fully melted, sealing the bubbles inside.
China's kilns cooled slowly, and crystals are thought to have grown.
Goryeo grew fewer crystals, and got bubbles in the trade.
Even the crackle webbing the surface started out as a flaw.
Clay and glaze shrink at different rates, so the skin splits open.
The old potters took even that flaw and called it decoration.
A Southern Song writer, Taepyeongnoin, called this color first under heaven.
A thousand-year-old blue, made without one drop of blue pigment.
