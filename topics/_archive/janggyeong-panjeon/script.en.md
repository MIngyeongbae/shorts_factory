# Janggyeong Panjeon — what lasted 700 years wasn't a mystery, it was windows and a floor

- 시드: https://namu.wiki/w/팔만대장경
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — the size gap between the south and north windows, the path the air takes, the charcoal and salt layers in a cutaway of the earth floor, the blocks warping once they were moved into concrete: none of it lands unless you draw it
- 핵심 질문: How did 80,000 wooden blocks — mold when damp, split when dry — last 700 years?
- 핵심 반전: The secret isn't a miracle. It's a ventilation scheme that sized the windows differently top to bottom and front to back, plus a floor packed with charcoal and salt. Two attempts to "improve" on it, with copper roof tiles and with concrete, were both reversed — proving the original design right the hard way
- 보이는 것: air moving in through the big low south windows → between the blocks → out the high north windows; the layers in a cutaway of the dug-up floor; a temperature graph swinging under copper tiles, and a block warped inside concrete
- 차별점: praise for traditional architecture usually comes with no test attached; this building has two failed control groups, in 1955 and 1972

## 대본

Wood rots. And yet there's wood here that has lasted 700 years.
80,000 printing blocks, shelved in the Janggyeong Panjeon at Haeinsa.
Damp, and wood molds. Dry, and it splits. That's wood.
Haeinsa burned seven times, and the blocks came through fine.
The secret? The Buddha's protection? No — window size.
On the south wall, the lower windows are about four times the upper ones.
On the north wall it's the opposite: up top is the big one.
Wind off the valley pushes in through those low south windows.
It was designed to run between the blocks and out the high north windows.
The whole building is a ventilation fan that needs no electricity.
The floor is better still: charcoal and salt, layered into packed earth.
Humid, and it drinks the moisture in. Dry, and it gives it back.
Twice, people who didn't buy this design stepped in.
In 1955, the clay tiles came off and gleaming copper went on.
The temperature inside started swinging, so the clay tiles went back.
In 1972, they built a concrete hall and moved a few blocks over.
The blocks warped. Everything went back to the wooden building.
What lasted 700 years wasn't a mystery. It was windows and a floor.

## 주장

- [줄 1·18] **Verified** — Work on the Tripitaka blocks began in 1236 (23rd year of Gojong of Goryeo) and finished in 1251 (38th year), which is 775 years as of 2026. "700 years" is a floor, not a count — [우리역사넷 「팔만대장경」](https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/kc/view.do?levelId=kc_r200500)
- [줄 2] **Verified** — The blocks are stored slotted into racks in the Janggyeong Panjeon at Haeinsa (four halls, including Sudarajang and Beopbojeon) — [국가유산포털](https://www.heritage.go.kr/heri/cul/culSelectDetail.do?ccbaCpno=1113800520000)
- [줄 2] **Verified** — A 2015 recount gives 81,352 blocks; the long-standing figure (since 1915) is 81,258. The script says "80,000" — [경향신문 「'팔만대장경'은 8만1352판이다」](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/201508100600025), [국가유산청](https://www.heritage.go.kr/heri/html/HtmlPage.do?pg=/unesco/Heritage/Heritage_02.jsp&pageNo=5_1_1_0)
- [줄 2] **Corrected** — Janggyeong Panjeon is a National Treasure (designated 1962.12.20) and a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1995. Note: the designation-number system ("National Treasure No. 52") was abolished in 2021, so current usage is simply "National Treasure" — [국가유산포털](https://www.heritage.go.kr/heri/cul/culSelectDetail.do?ccbaCpno=1113800520000)
- [줄 4] **Verified** — Haeinsa suffered seven fires over 176 years, from 1695 (21st year of Sukjong) to 1871 (8th year of Gojong), with no damage to the depositories or the Tripitaka — [정책브리핑](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=70087256)
- [줄 4] **Likely** — That the depositories' siting and design helped them survive the fires is an explanation, not a measurement. The script states the two facts side by side without claiming causation, so it stands as written — [정책브리핑](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=70087256)
- [줄 5~7] **Verified** — The depositories were built with lattice windows of differing size front to back (south/north) and top to bottom, for airflow — [국가유산청 「조선 대기과학 연구의 상징, 합천 해인사 장경판전」](https://www.cha.go.kr/cop/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?nttId=73548&bbsId=BBSMSTR_1008&pageUnit=10&searchtitle=title&mn=NS_01_09_01)
- [줄 6] **Verified** — On the south (front) side, the lower lattice window is **about 4 times** the upper one — [국가유산청](https://www.cha.go.kr/cop/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?nttId=73548&bbsId=BBSMSTR_1008&pageUnit=10&searchtitle=title&mn=NS_01_09_01), [경향신문](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/201801232139015)
- [줄 7] **Verified** — On the north (rear) side, the upper window is **about 1.5 times** the lower one. The script speaks only the south ratio and leaves the north ratio to an on-screen label — [국가유산청](https://www.cha.go.kr/cop/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?nttId=73548&bbsId=BBSMSTR_1008&pageUnit=10&searchtitle=title&mn=NS_01_09_01)
- [줄 6~7 on-screen label] **Verified** — Front lower window: 2.5m wide × 1.0m high; upper window: 1.2m wide — [정책브리핑](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=70087256)
- [줄 8] **Verified** — The terrain is high and closed to the north, open low to the south, so wind moves from the low south side toward the north — [경향신문](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/201801232139015)
- [줄 9] **Likely → rewritten as design intent** — Sources describe air passing between the racks and blocks and out the high north windows as the **intent of the design** ("carries this intent" / "presumed to have been designed so"); the actual interior wind speed and airflow only entered scientific testing in 2018. The script says "was designed to run … out" rather than asserting measured flow — [국가유산청](https://www.cha.go.kr/cop/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?nttId=73548&bbsId=BBSMSTR_1008&pageUnit=10&searchtitle=title&mn=NS_01_09_01), [경향신문](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/201801232139015)
- [줄 10] **Verified** — A building optimized for preserving the blocks, with natural ventilation and no electrical equipment — [국가유산청](https://www.cha.go.kr/cop/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?nttId=73548&bbsId=BBSMSTR_1008&pageUnit=10&searchtitle=title&mn=NS_01_09_01)
- [줄 11] **Verified** — Charcoal, lime, and salt were laid into the earth floor in sequence along with sand (loess) to regulate humidity. The script names only charcoal and salt; lime and sand are carried by an on-screen label — [위키백과 합천 해인사 장경판전](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/합천_해인사_장경판전), [국가유산청 「기억의 보존과 지속성」](https://www.khs.go.kr/cop/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?nttId=92725&bbsId=BBSMSTR_1008&pageUnit=10&searchtitle=title&mn=NS_01_09_01)
- [줄 12] **Verified** — The floor absorbs moisture when the air is humid and releases it when the air is dry, regulating humidity automatically. "Drinks it in and gives it back" is a figurative simplification of absorption and release — [뉴시스](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20110715_0008688714)
- [줄 13] **Verified** — There were two deliberate interventions, in 1955 and 1972 — [이코리아](https://www.ekoreanews.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=25686)
- [줄 14] **Verified** — In 1955 the old roof tiles were stripped off the depositories and copper tiles laid in their place — [이코리아](https://www.ekoreanews.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=25686)
- [줄 15] **Unsupported → corrected** — "Dew formed on the blocks and they rotted" cannot be confirmed outside the Namuwiki seed. The reason the press gives for the reversal is that copper's high thermal conductivity made the temperature swing inside the building. That clause is replaced with "the temperature inside started swinging" — [이코리아](https://www.ekoreanews.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=25686)
- [줄 15] **Verified** — The copper tiles were removed and the original clay tiles restored — [이코리아](https://www.ekoreanews.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=25686)
- [줄 16] **Corrected** — In 1972, by presidential order, a two-story concrete building was **newly built alongside** the existing halls. The claim that the wooden depositories were demolished is false; the script's wording ("they built a concrete hall") was already accurate — [이코리아](https://www.ekoreanews.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=25686), [뉴시스](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20110715_0008688714)
- [줄 16~17] **Corrected** — Not a full transfer: "a few blocks were moved as a test and developed warping and condensation," after which they went back to the wooden depositories. "A few blocks" is added to line 16 for precision — [이코리아](https://www.ekoreanews.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=25686)
- [줄 18] **Verified** — The current depositories were rebuilt in 1457 (3rd year of Sejo) and 1488 (19th year of Seongjong), roughly 540 years ago. The blocks' 775 years and the building's lifespan differ, but the script's subject is the blocks ("what lasted 700 years"), so it isn't an error. The gap is filed in the twist notes as separate hook material — [위키백과](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/합천_해인사_장경판전), [국가유산포털](https://www.heritage.go.kr/heri/cul/culSelectDetail.do?ccbaCpno=1113800520000)
