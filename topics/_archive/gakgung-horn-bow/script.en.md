# The gakgung — Joseon's greatest bow, with an imported heart

- 시드: https://namu.wiki/w/각궁

- 핵심 질문: It was Joseon's signature weapon, so why could it never be made entirely inside Joseon?
- 핵심 반전: The heart of the gakgung, a symbol of tradition, was entirely imported water buffalo horn, and Ming China classed that horn as military materiel and banned its export, holding Joseon's bow production in its grip
- 보이는 것: the layered cross-section of the bow → a map where the sea route for horn is cut → buffalo multiplying in the palace grounds, then drifting away to islands

## 대본

Joseon's finest weapon was the one thing Joseon couldn't make itself.
The gakgung is a bow: wood, with ox sinew and horn layered onto it.
The sinew on the back stretches, the horn inside compresses, and force builds.
So the same pull sent an arrow far, far further downrange.
The catch is that horn. No water buffalo lived in Joseon.
Every piece of it had to be bought from China, Japan, or Ryukyu.
The heart of the bow was parked in someone else's warehouse.
Ming China called the horn military materiel and blocked it from leaving.
Whether Joseon could make bows at all sat in Ming's hands.
Joseon didn't just take it. It decided to raise the buffalo at home.
Under Sejo, a breeding pair arrived from Ryukyu and the herd began.
Growth was slow: by Seongjong's reign it had reached seventy head.
But buffalo handed out to households started goring people.
They were pushed off to islands, and the home-grown plan ended there.
Deer antler and ox horn were tried as substitutes.
Too short, too weak — they never filled the water buffalo's place.
So Joseon pooled money into guilds and bought horn from Japan instead.
The bow we call a tradition was, in truth, centuries of imports.
