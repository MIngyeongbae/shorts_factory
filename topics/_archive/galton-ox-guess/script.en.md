# The ox's weight was guessed not by an expert, but by 787 wrong answers

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisdom_of_the_crowd
- 매체 적합성: Fits — 787 slips scattered along a number line, the point at their center sitting almost exactly on the true-weight line; then, once people confer, the points clump to one side and the true-weight line gets pushed out past the edge of the spread. The picture shows the causation directly.
- 핵심 질문: Nobody in the crowd knows the answer, so why does adding 787 of them together produce it?
- 핵심 반전: The accuracy comes not from skill but from the errors pointing in every direction — which is why talking it over pushes the answer further away.
- 보이는 것: 787 scattered guesses (1074–1293 pounds) → the median lands almost exactly on the real weight → scales with mis-set dials stack up and converge on the true value → after consensus, the spread leans one way and the true-weight line ends up at the margin
- 차별점: Not a feel-good "crowds are wise" story, but a single causal thread from the conditions that make the effect work (independence, diversity) to the ones that break it (consensus, shared bias)
- 여운: Unanimity is the sign that diversity has died.

## 대본

Can 787 people who don't know the answer arrive at the answer?
A country fair in Plymouth, England, 1906.
The contest: guess the weight of an ox, slaughtered and dressed.
787 people wrote down a number, and the guesses were wildly far apart.
The statistician Francis Galton collected every single ticket.
He lined them up smallest to largest and picked the one in the middle.
1208 pounds. The ox actually weighed 1197 pounds.
Off by under one percent. And nobody there knew the answer.
The trick isn't skill, it's direction. Some guess high, some guess low.
When the errors point every which way, adding them up cancels them out.
787 scales, every dial off by a different amount. Trust one, no. Stack them, yes.
But let people peek at each other's answers, and the magic breaks.
In an ETH Zürich experiment, letting people confer pushed the truth out to the edge of the spread.
"The more opinions diverge, the smaller the error." That's scholar Scott Page's formula.
Though if all 787 lean wrong the same way, the average is wrong too.
What guessed the ox's weight wasn't an expert. It was 787 different wrong answers.

- [줄 9~10] **확인** — Idiosyncratic noise, which points a different way for each person, averages out across many responses. Seed lead paragraph and "Challenges" section

- [줄 12] **확인** — The effect presupposes that individual judgments are independent. Seed lead paragraph, "Challenges" section
- [줄 13] **확인** — Experiment at ETH Zürich. Lorenz, Rauhut, Schweitzer & Helbing (2011), PNAS 108(22):9020–9025, N=144. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1008636108
- [줄 13] **정정('정확도가 떨어졌다'→'정답이 분포 끝으로 밀렸다')** — The paper's conclusion is not a drop in accuracy but that diversity shrinks "without improvements of its collective error." - [줄 14] **확인** — Scott Page's diversity prediction theorem: collective squared error = average individual squared error − prediction diversity. Seed "Challenges" section. (Caveat: it is a mathematical identity, so reading it as "raise diversity alone and error falls" overstates it)
- [줄 15] **정정(800명→787명)** — The claim itself checks out. Averaging erases only the random error that varies person to person; it cannot erase systematic error or cognitive bias that the whole crowd shares in the same direction. Seed "Challenges" section
