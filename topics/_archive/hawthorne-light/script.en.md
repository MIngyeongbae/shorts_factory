# They dimmed the lights and output went up — the Hawthorne raw data that broke the story

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — the illumination dial, two output curves side by side, and a reel of microfilm put the study design and its reversal straight on screen
- 핵심 질문: In a plant where output rose whether the lights went up or down, which variable was actually doing the work?
- 핵심 반전: The illumination study that textbooks cite as proof that "being watched raises performance" ended with the opposite verdict — light was irrelevant — and a 2011 reanalysis of the long-buried raw data found the effect itself was faint
- 보이는 것: test room and control room side by side, illumination moving in opposite directions while both output lines rise together; the break point down at moonlight; a microfilm reel and the reanalysis graph; men easing off in front of a wage table
- 차별점: a famous law that vanishes inside the very experiment cited as its evidence — and, in the same plant, an observation study where performance actually dropped
- 여운: where the data goes missing, the story stays

## 대본

They turned the factory lights down, and output went up instead.
Hawthorne Works, 1924. Would brighter light mean more parts made?
One room where the light changed, one where it didn't. Side by side.
They raised the lights, and the test room's output climbed.
But the room next door, where nothing changed, climbed too.
So they dimmed the lights instead — and both rooms rose again.
Only at moonlight, when hands disappeared, did output start to fall.
The researchers' conclusion was this. Brightness had nothing to do with it.
So what lifted it? The watching eye became the suspect.
The name pinned on that: the Hawthorne effect, a textbook staple.
Yet nobody had ever analyzed the raw data from that study.
Levitt and List, two economists, finally tracked it down years later.
It was sitting on microfilm at the University of Wisconsin.
Recrunched in 2011, the effect was faint. The legend was bigger.
At the same plant, a study watching men went the opposite way.
Work fast, the base rate might drop — so they slowed down on purpose.
The same gaze can be a cheering section, or a security camera.
One thing is certain. What moved that factory wasn't the light.
