## 대본

A £20,000 prize, and the man who solved it never collected it.
18th-century ships could find north and south, but not east and west.
In 1707, four warships went down off the Isles of Scilly.
1,400 died, so in 1714 Parliament put up £20,000.
The answer came not from a scholar but a country carpenter, John Harrison.
Here's how. You bottle the time you set sail, and carry it with you.
If it's noon here and your clock says two, you're two hours west.
A rolling ship, salt, temperature. Even Newton thought it couldn't be built.
Harrison spent 40 years, fusing two metals to tame the temperature.
In 1761, H4 — about the size of a pocket watch — sailed for Jamaica.
81 days later, 5 seconds off. On the map, one nautical mile — nearly in port.
But the Board called it luck, and held the prize back.
It passed the retest too — three times sharper than the lunar method.
But Maskelyne, who backed that lunar method, took a seat on the Board.
His report was negative. H4 won twice, and still failed.
Finally, at 80, the King stepped in, and £8,750 came through.
The £20,000, in the end, went to no one at all.
