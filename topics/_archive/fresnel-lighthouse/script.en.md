# Why a lighthouse was visible 32 kilometres out — it was the glass, not the lamp

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresnel_lens
- 매체 적합성: Fits — light spraying in every direction, a lens sliced into concentric rings, a path bending inside a prism: all of it draws as lines and arrows, and the drawing is the explanation
- 핵심 질문: How did a 200-year-old lamp throw light 32 kilometres?
- 핵심 반전: They didn't turn the brightness up — they redirected the light that was being thrown away. And the famous stepped lens was Buffon's first, while the outer prisms, Fresnel's own invention, were only proposed after the 32-kilometre record
- 보이는 것: lamp light spraying in all directions → a lens cross-section cut into concentric rings like tree rings → the Arc de Triomphe seen from 32 kilometres away → the beam bouncing inside the later outer prisms and merging into one horizontal line
- 차별점: the stepped pattern everyone pictures when they hear "Fresnel lens" wasn't actually his, and the outer prism rings that were his came three years after the 32-kilometre record — a double reversal

## 대본

A lighthouse beam reaches 32 kilometres. Is that because the lamp is bright?
The real problem was never brightness — it was where the light went.
Light a lamp, and the light scatters in every direction.
But sailors are only ever on the horizon, one thin line.
Back then mirrors gathered the light, and the mirrors swallowed half of it.
In 1819, French physicist Fresnel was handed the problem.
His answer: slice the glass into concentric rings, like tree rings.
Throw the thickness away, keep only the angle that bends the light.
But that stepped lens wasn't Fresnel's idea first.
A commissioner pointed out that Buffon had proposed it 70 years earlier.
1822: the Arc de Triomphe test. Louis XVIII saw the light 32 km away.
The next year Cordouan lighthouse was lit, and the light reached the horizon.
But the light leaking above and below was still being swallowed by mirrors.
Fresnel's own invention came next, on the outside of the lens.
In 1825, prisms instead of mirrors. The light bounces off the inner wall of the glass.
Light that was escaping turns and joins the horizontal beam.
It wasn't a brighter lamp — it was glass that won back the leaking light.
