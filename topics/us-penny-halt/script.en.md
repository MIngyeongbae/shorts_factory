# 3.7 Cents to Make a Penny — The Day America Stopped Minting

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_(United_States_coin)
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — a bar comparison of 3.7 cents in cost against 1 cent in face value, arrows running mint → wallet → drawer with nothing coming back, a register rounding to 5 cents, and an auction hammer price: the screen does the explaining
- 핵심 질문: A coin with 250 billion of them out there — why did it vanish from the checkout?
- 핵심 반전: Not because there weren't enough. The penny only ever made a one-way trip from the Mint to your wallet, so it had to be struck again and again at a loss just to keep up
- 보이는 것: The height gap between cost and face value, a circulation diagram missing its return arrow, a jar packed with coins, a rounding notice, an auction room
- 차별점: The coin that was stopped because it lost money sold for 800,000 dollars the moment it stopped

## 대본

It cost 3.7 cents to make a single penny
Each one lost money, so production for circulation stopped in 2025
But the strange part comes right after that
An estimated 250 billion pennies had already been struck
That works out to more than 700 for every American
And still, pennies disappeared from the checkout
So let's follow where this coin actually goes
A penny travels from the Mint to your wallet, and stops
It piles up in drawers, in jars, in car cup holders
A 1995 study found two out of three never came back
It was a rail line selling tickets with no train coming back
So the Mint had to strike billions more every single year
In 2024 alone, the loss came to 85 million dollars
Then the Federal Reserve stopped taking pennies as well
Stores began rounding cash totals to the nearest 5 cents
Today, 20 states have made that rounding legal
Then the last three pennies ever struck went to auction
The winning bid: 800,000 dollars — for a 3.7-cent coin
