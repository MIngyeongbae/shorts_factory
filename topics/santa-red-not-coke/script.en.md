# Santa Wore Red Before Coca-Cola

- 시드: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Claus
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — Comparing Santa illustrations and ads chronologically reveals the history of his red suit
- 핵심 질문: Did Coca-Cola really create Santa’s red suit?
- 핵심 반전: A mineral water ad came before Coca-Cola, and red-suited Santa appeared even earlier on magazine covers
- 보이는 것: A timeline moving backward from Coca-Cola ads to a 1915 mineral water ad, early-1900s magazines, and 19th-century illustrations
- 차별점: Instead of naming one originator, it traces how different media gradually established Santa’s appearance

## 대본

Did a cola company really put Santa in that red suit?
Then what color was Santa before the cola ads?
Coca-Cola’s illustrations became hugely famous in the 1930s.
That even sparked the claim that its brand colors became Santa’s outfit.
But run the timeline backward, and the story changes.
First, look at White Rock’s 1915 mineral water ad.
It’s monochrome, yet Santa already wears an outfit much like today’s.
From 1923, the same company’s ads even show the colors.
A red suit with white fur, several years before Coca-Cola.
But we need to turn back one more page.
Red Santa also appeared on Puck magazine covers in the early 1900s.
So advertisers didn’t reinvent Santa; they inherited his image.
Its roots go deeper, into 19th-century illustrations and poetry.
Nast turned an elf-like Santa into a full-sized adult.
It was as if different artists kept updating the same character.
So cola didn’t put Santa into a red suit.
It made the already-red Santa into a face known worldwide.
