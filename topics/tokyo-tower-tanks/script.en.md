# Why was the top of Tokyo Tower built with tank steel?

- 시드: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東京タワー
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — The material’s transformation can be visualized from tank dismantling through steelmaking and beam production to assembly atop the tower.
- 핵심 질문: How did US military tanks used in war become part of Tokyo’s symbol?
- 핵심 반전: The tank steel wasn’t used throughout the tower. It was concentrated above the former Main Observatory, now the 150m Main Deck.
- 보이는 것: Scattered broadcast towers, dismantled tanks, reprocessed steel, and a path leading into Tokyo Tower’s upper structure
- 차별점: The story of roughly 90 tanks that once crossed the ground becoming the frame of a tower that sent out broadcasts

## 대본
If you split Tokyo Tower into top and bottom, does its steel have two different pasts?
Why is a trace of war hidden inside this red-and-white landmark?
In 1950s Tokyo, each broadcaster had its own transmission tower.
Every time you changed channels, you had to turn your home antenna too.
What Tokyo needed was one far-reaching tower, taller than the Eiffel Tower.
But postwar Japan was short of high-quality structural steel.
Then US military tanks scrapped after the Korean War entered the scrap market.
Selling the old M4s and M47s made more sense than repairing them.
The tanks were dismantled, and Tokyo Steel turned their iron into structural sections.
Their armor was melted down into the bones of a giant antenna.
But that steel wasn’t used across the entire 4,000-ton tower.
It went above the former Main Observatory, today’s Main Deck.
Steel from roughly 90 tanks now supports the sky above Tokyo.
The 333-meter tower was completed in 1958, just 543 days after construction began.
Tokyo’s scattered broadcast signals began converging on this single tower.
So when you look up at Tokyo Tower, try to find the boundary.
Above it stands the second life of 90 tanks that once crossed the ground.
