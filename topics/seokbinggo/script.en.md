## 대본

Would you believe Joseon Korea ate ice in midsummer, with no electricity?
And not just any ice: winter ice, kept frozen for over six months.
The secret lies in the seokbinggo, an ice storehouse built of stone.
But despite the name, stone isn't what saved the ice.
Half of it sat underground, like a cellar that stays cool all summer.
The ceiling was an arch, with vents at the very top.
Warm air rises, so it slipped out through those vents by itself.
Cold air is heavy, so it stayed low, right beside the ice.
A floor drain tilted at 5 degrees carried meltwater straight out.
So what really kept the ice? Rice straw.
They packed straw tight around it. Straw insulates about as well as styrofoam.
A puffer jacket for ice, basically. And the numbers are striking.
Fill it halfway, wrap the ice in straw, and after six months only 0.4% melts.
Fill it to the top, and even with straw, 18% melts. Greed costs you.
That's how river ice over 12 cm thick lasted from midwinter to the next summer.
Summer ice without electricity: they had it figured out 300 years ago.

## 주장

- [줄 1] Joseon stored winter ice until summer without any mechanical device (distributed and used from the spring equinox to Sanggang, late October) — Confirmed. 우리역사넷 「석빙고」 https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/tt/view.do?levelId=tt_b30 ; 대한민국 정책브리핑 「한국적이면서도 과학적인 석빙고」(이종호, 2004.11.12) https://www.korea.kr/briefing/policyBriefingView.do?newsId=65081691
- [줄 2] Ice was cut in the 12th lunar month and used from the spring equinox to Sanggang, so storage exceeded six months — Confirmed. 우리역사넷 "work done every year in the 12th lunar month when the weather is quite cold" · "used from the spring equinox to Sanggang"
- [줄 3] The seokbinggo is an ice storehouse built of dressed granite laid as an arch (hongye) — Confirmed. 국가유산청 월간국가유산사랑 「조선의 냉장고 '석빙고의 과학'」 https://www.khs.go.kr/cop/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?nttId=74868&bbsId=BBSMSTR_1008
- [줄 4] The variable that determined ice loss was straw, not stone — Confirmed (interpretation). 정책브리핑, calculation by 장동순: no straw 38.4%/51.8% vs. with straw 0.4%/18.4% (six months, 50%/100% fill)
- [줄 5] The ice chamber is a half-underground structure, half below and half above ground, minimizing outside temperature swings — Confirmed. 정책브리핑 "half underground, the other half above ground"; 국가유산청 "a half-underground structure with small temperature variation"
- [줄 6] The ceiling is arched with 2–3 vents at the top (3 at the Gyeongju seokbinggo, about 30×30 cm) — Confirmed. 국가유산청 "3 ventilation holes"; 정책브리핑 "2–3 vents, generally 30×30 centimeters"
- [줄 7] Warm air from radiant heat and from the entrance escapes through the ceiling vents — Confirmed. 국가유산청 「냉동 원리」, verbatim
- [줄 8] Because warm air escapes, the cold air at the bottom of the chamber lingers and less ice melts — Confirmed. 국가유산청 "the cold air at the bottom of the ice chamber can stay for a long time"
- [줄 9] A drain channel was cut down the center of the floor at a 5° slope so meltwater flows out — Confirmed (correction applied: the 5° is the slope of the drain channel, not the whole floor). 국가유산청 "a drain channel dug at a 5° slope down the center of the interior floor" — on-screen label "5° sloped drain" https://www.khs.go.kr/cop/bbs/selectBoardArticle.do?nttId=74868&bbsId=BBSMSTR_1008
- [줄 10] The space between the ice and the walls/ceiling was filled with rice husks, straw, reeds, etc. for insulation — Confirmed. 국가유산청 "stored packed with rice husks or straw"; 정책브리핑 "rice straw or reeds"; 우리역사넷 "15,000 bundles of reeds … covered the ice house above, below, and on all sides"
- [줄 11] Rice straw has a thermal conductivity of k=0.04 W/m℃, similar to styrofoam (k=0.03 W/m℃) — Confirmed. 정책브리핑 「단열 원리」 — on-screen label "rice straw 0.04 / styrofoam 0.03 W/m℃" https://www.korea.kr/briefing/policyBriefingView.do?newsId=65081691
- [줄 12] The loss-rate figures are a calculated analysis by Professor 장동순 of Chungnam National University, not measurements — Confirmed. 정책브리핑 "analyzed the calculation results"
- [줄 13] With 50% ice fill and straw insulation, ice loss after six months is 0.4% (0.04% after three months); without straw, 38.4% (6.4% after three months) — Confirmed (correction applied: the source never says "the other half was filled with straw," so the line was changed to "wrap the ice in straw"). 정책브리핑 original text — on-screen label "50% fill + straw: 0.4%" https://www.korea.kr/briefing/policyBriefingView.do?newsId=65081691
- [줄 14] With 100% ice fill and straw, loss after six months is 18.4% (2.8% after three months); without straw, 51.8% (9.2% after three months) — Confirmed (correction applied: 18% is the with-straw condition, so the line was changed to "even with straw, 18%"). 정책브리핑 original text — on-screen label "100% fill + straw: 18.4% / no straw: 51.8%" https://www.korea.kr/briefing/policyBriefingView.do?newsId=65081691
- [줄 15] Ice was cut only when at least 4 chi (about 12 cm) thick, from rivers in deep winter (12th lunar month) — Confirmed. 정책브리핑 "must be 12 centimeters or thicker"; 우리역사넷 "4치(약 12m) 이상" (12m is a typo for 12cm; 1 chi ≈ 3.03 cm) · "cut ice from the frozen-solid river" — on-screen label "12cm" https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/tt/view.do?levelId=tt_b30
- [줄 16] The surviving seokbinggo date from the 18th century — Cheongdo 1713 (Sukjong year 39), Hyeonpung 1730, Andong 1737, Gyeongju 1738 (Yeongjo year 14), Changnyeong 1742 → roughly 300 years as of 2026 — Confirmed. 위키백과 「석빙고」 https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EC%84%9D%EB%B9%99%EA%B3%A0 ; 우리역사넷 "the Cheongdo seokbinggo … Sukjong year 39 (1713)"; 국가유산청 "Yeongjo year 14 (1738)"
