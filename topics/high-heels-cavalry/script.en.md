## 대본

Why were high heels made so difficult to walk in?
If they were always about style, that seems strange.
The secret isn’t in a ballroom, but on a galloping horse.
In the 10th century, Persian cavalry wore heeled boots.
Hooked into a stirrup, the heel braced the foot from behind.
So even at a gallop, the foot didn’t slide forward.
It also made it easier to stand in the stirrups and shoot a bow.
In other words, an awkward heel anchored the body in battle.
That same function survives in cowboy boots today.
Then, in the early 17th century, Persian emissaries came to Europe.
European men turned the cavalry heel into a mark of power.
After all, riding horses was a costly, time-consuming privilege.
Royalty like Louis XIV wore heels and flaunted their height.
As women adopted them, heel styles split by gender in the 18th century.
Men wore thick heels; women’s became thin and high.
Before high heels signaled femininity, they were cavalry safety gear.
