# Why Did Seokguram Lose Its Breath After Restoration?

- 시드: https://namu.wiki/w/석굴암
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — The grotto’s assembled structure, water channels, condensation, concrete domes, and glass wall can all be visualized.
- 핵심 질문: Why did Seokguram, which survived for over 1,000 years, become unable to endure without machinery after restoration?
- 핵심 반전: The concrete added for protection and the rerouted water worsened its moisture problem.
- 보이는 것: A grotto once ventilated by spring water and air becomes encased in concrete and sealed shut.
- 차별점: The paradox of a restoration that changed not only the monument’s physical structure, but also how people worship there.

## 대본
Could a restoration meant to save a monument become its prison instead?
Today, Seokguram sits behind glass doors, surrounded by mechanically driven air.
Yet this grotto endured in the mountains for over 1,000 years, starting in the 8th century.
It isn’t carved from solid rock, either. It’s an artificial grotto assembled from granite pieces.
The trouble began when the collapsed grotto was repaired in the 1910s.
Its exterior was wrapped in concrete, the cutting-edge material of the time.
It was like adding a raincoat that also trapped all the sweat inside.
The spring water beneath the floor was also blamed for the moisture and diverted.
One theory says that water cooled the floor, drawing condensation downward.
But after the water flow and ventilation changed, condensation spread across the walls and statues.
Steam removed the moss, but it also wore away the sculptures’ surfaces.
After liberation, another concrete dome was built over the first.
As moisture worsened, the grotto was sealed and air conditioning was installed.
The worship path around the main Buddha also came to an end at the glass wall.
Preservation now means keeping people away and machinery running constantly.
Why can Seokguram, after enduring 1,000 years, no longer breathe on its own after restoration?
