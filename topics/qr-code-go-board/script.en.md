# The QR code — a square born on a Go board, and the patent nobody had to pay for

- 시드: https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/QR_%EC%BD%94%EB%93%9C
- 매체 적합성: Suitable — black and white stones on a Go board hardening into a grid, a scan line crossing a corner square and measuring the 1:1:3:1:1 widths, a torn code still reading fine: each shot explains itself
- 핵심 질문: Why doesn't the company behind the square the whole world scans every day collect a fee for it?
- 핵심 반전: The three squares in the corners aren't a design choice — they're the black-and-white ratio that a survey found least often in printed matter. And the invention was patented, then released anyway
- 보이는 것: Barcode labels stacked on a parts box → black and white stones on a Go board → a grid of code → a scan line that returns the same width ratio from any angle → a smeared code being restored on screen
- 차별점: The motive for the invention (ten barcodes on one parts box), how the ratio was found, the patents that were never enforced, and the ending in Korea where the name got too common to register as a trademark

## 대본

You scan this little square every day — so why doesn't the company that made it charge you?
It starts in 1994, at a car parts factory in Japan.
A single box could carry a whole stack of barcode labels.
Workers had to scan the same box about ten times over.
At Denso, Masahiro Hara found his answer on a Go board.
Black stones and white stones on a grid — that was the whole idea.
Writing that used to fill one line now spread across a full page.
The problem was where a camera should start reading that board.
So the team ran a survey of the world's printed matter.
Hunting for the black-and-white ratio that text and pictures almost never make.
The answer was 1 to 1 to 3 to 1 to 1 — those three squares in the corners.
Sweep from any direction, hit that ratio, and you've found a QR code.
Turn the error correction all the way up and 30 percent can be ruined.
Denso Wave, as it later became, held the patents and said: follow the standard, pay nothing.
So the whole world took it. Payments, menus, vaccine certificates.
In Korea, even the QR Code trademark was refused. Too common a word.
A square born on a Go board gave up the money and got the world instead.

## 주장

- [줄 2] The QR code was invented in 1994 at Denso, a car parts company in Aichi, Japan — 기사 「역사」 / ja.wikipedia 「QRコード」 (확인)
- [줄 3] Before the invention, boxes of car parts carried several separate barcodes — 기사 「역사」 (확인)
- [줄 4] Around ten barcodes were lined up and scanned for parts tracking, and workers on the floor complained it was exhausting — ja.wikipedia 「QRコード」 / 덴소 웨이브 기술자료(하루 약 1,000회 스캔) (정정 반영: 기존 "두 번, 세 번"은 근거 없음)
- [줄 4~7] Consolidating those multiple barcodes into a single label to track part types and quantities was the functional purpose of the QR code — 기사 「역사」 (확인)
- [줄 5] Masahiro Hara led the research team, and the black-and-white square design was inspired by the arrangement of black and white stones on a Go board — 기사 「역사」 (확인). Note that at the time of development he worked in Denso's development division; Denso Wave is a subsidiary spun off in October 2001 (정정 반영)
- [줄 7] The QR code is a two-dimensional matrix barcode, holding data along both axes, so it stores more than a one-dimensional barcode — 기사 첫 문단, 「디자인」 (확인)
- [줄 8] One-dimensional barcodes are scanned with a narrow beam of light, while a QR code is captured by a two-dimensional image sensor and then analyzed by a processor — 기사 「디자인」 (확인)
- [줄 9~10] The finder pattern's proportions were set by an exhaustive survey of the ratio of white to black areas least used in printed matter such as leaflets, magazines and corrugated cartons — 기사 「역사」 / 덴소 웨이브 기술자료 (확인)
- [줄 11] That ratio is 1:1:3:1:1, and the finder patterns sit at three corners of the code (upper left, upper right, lower left) — 기사 「역사」, 「디자인」 (확인)
- [줄 12] The processor locates these finder patterns to normalize the code's size, orientation and viewing angle, so it reads from any direction in 360° — 기사 「디자인」 / qrcode.com (확인)
- [줄 13] It uses Reed–Solomon error correction, recovering up to 30% of the data bytes at the highest level H (L 7%, M 15%, Q 25%) — 기사 「오류 정정」 (확인)
- [줄 14] Denso Wave held numerous patents on QR code technology but waived its key patent rights for standardized codes. US patent 5726435 expired on 14 March 2015 and Japanese patent 2938338 on 14 March 2014 — 기사 「라이선스」 (확인)
- [줄 15] QR codes are used for payments, restaurant menu ordering, COVID-19 vaccination certificates and more — 기사 「QR 코드 결제」, 「식당 주문」, 「코로나19 범유행」 (확인)
- [줄 16] In South Korea, a QR Code trademark application filed on 18 November 2011 was refused on 20 March 2012 because the patent office judged it a generic term for matrix barcodes in general. "QR Code" nonetheless remains a registered trademark of Denso Wave — 기사 「라이선스」 (확인)
